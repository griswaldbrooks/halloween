## What is ready

Nine abstract vector rooms form a snake in storyboard order: arrival/register, tutorial, sight, hearing, touch, speech, direct contact, false choice, reveal/exit. Four named tokens move through doors. Player cards show sight/hearing/speech/hands, offerings in physical custody, healer and Informer overlays, and removal. ## Findings

- The baseline cannot meet 20 minutes: scenes sum to **24 minutes**, plus **one minute movement**, plus **one minute per sacrifice**. Tested outcomes: four finale guests 25 minutes; three 26; two 27; one 28. These are estimates, not measured rehearsals or a real-time stopwatch. Operator choice/healer time is included in scene budgets. Review log line 9 says the 2025 retros have no measured per-group runtime; there is no historical validation.
- Even five failures can keep all four guests: four offering payments plus one eligible curse. Therefore the proposed economy does not force a removal. All four still become “hers” in the proposed ending.
- **The Named fallback is unreachable in the current baseline.** Reaching one player costs three of five failures, leaving at most two payments; exhausting four offerings would require four more failures. It remains visible as a draft/hypothetical branch, disabled under baseline conditions. Repeated Named payment after surrendering the name token has no specified rule. This would matter in an expanded or retuned game, not the current five-test run.
- Temporary curses do not disappear on payment. They remain through the next full challenge, unless the living healer spends the one cure first. Veiled persists to the guest's personal ending and confers no protection from sacrifice.
- A physical removal requires a real pause: both collectors enter, Captain returns to the waiting group, Tamari finishes the exit. The page represents this separately from puzzle progress; it does not pretend two performers can escort and simultaneously run another challenge.
- Direct contact, arrival and reveal are scripted scenes. The storyboard specifies no pass/fail or cost there. The page identifies that missing failure branch rather than adding a new toll.
- The October 1 notes are newer than storyboard 07: immediate blindness and mid-game sacrifice became “maybes”; the spy became the Informer. This simulation deliberately exercises the requested revision 07 mechanics and labels the conflict instead of claiming final approval. It does not repeat the obsolete Tory-agent backstory or draft death incidents.

## Assumptions and rules needing tuning

1. One active ability curse per guest is a conservative compatibility default. An uncursed guest may draw Veiled, Deafened, Silenced or No Hands. The provided solo/alternate routes remain usable. This does not prove every possible stacked-curse combination; stacking is disabled.
2. Choose a recipient, then draw uniformly from eligible curse types. The storyboard mentions compatible shuffled cards without a full deck composition or recipient-selection procedure. Manual overrides inspect branches rather than pretending to be actual chance outcomes.
3. Four offerings are shared; Alex initially carries the purse. Custody transfers on removal. Everyone may spend from it. Seals and keepsakes are always in the shared basket; the page does not invent personal clue hoarding merely to animate a transfer.
4. Distinct healer and Informer overlays are randomly shuffled onto two of four guests. The spy's wrong suggestion has no automatic mechanical penalty; the operator records its effect through real pass/fail. Healer cure is lost upon removal. These are draft rules, not approved balance.
5. Eight moves divide the one-minute transition allowance into 7.5 seconds each. Escort is a one-minute event. Actual room geometry, accessibility and participant pace are unknown.
6. “Become hers,” ambiguously, is the working ending. Survivor count means people reaching the finale, not souls escaping the fiction. Removed guests get personal endings and return to the party; finale guests hear their names, receive tactile tokens, and remove curses only when the game ends.
7. No real house, optional Ouija, build selection, new fiction or revised storyboard is introduced. A 20-minute version would need cuts or revised timings; this simulation does not decide those cuts.
