**Session:** October 16, 2026  
**Purpose:** Faithful snapshot of decisions and open questions from the continued design conversation.  
**Status key:** **Committed** = explicitly accepted; **Working** = favored scenario, subject to refinement; **Open** = not yet decided.

## Core design principles — committed
- Design the **historical events and present-day player interactions together**, not as separate timelines patched together.
- Four main child encounters, in this order: **Waitstill → Deliverance → Mindwell → Remember**.
- Every encounter must be a **playable game**, not just exposition, and grounded in what its character historically did.
- Four puzzle families: **logic, observation, hunt/search, codebreaking**. Hybrids are welcome.
- Keep puzzles simpler than designers instinctively expect; each encounter should teach a pattern players can use later.
- The players must have real agency in solving puzzles, while it remains **ambiguous whether they alter the haunting or merely reenact a historical sequence**.
- Ghosts might be spirits, place-bound recordings or memories, or something between; do not settle the metaphysics.

## Physical staging — committed
- The main game occurs **downstairs**, with a **central séance table** serving as the hub for reports to Constance.
- Literal lighting cues direct attention to different activity areas; these are not necessarily separate rooms.
- **Waitstill** involves looking through a **spyglass** at multiple possible signals in the yard, selecting the correct one using a clue, and sending a matching reply.
- **Deliverance** is encountered via the outside signaling sequence. Players **go into the actual backyard**, search for the hidden physical warning (scroll/parchment), and **bring it back to the séance table**. Backyard is the chosen design, not an indoor window-box substitute. A bad-weather contingency remains to be designed.
- **Mindwell** is encountered downstairs, after the message is retrieved.
- After Mindwell's revelation, Constance's rage produces an apparent supernatural fire; players transition to the **garage**, the one major change of physical space.
- **Remember and the final decoding happen in the garage**, not Mindwell.

## Characters and intelligence roles — committed
- **Josiah:** magistrate, secretly aligned with the Patriot spy network; sends an **encrypted warning** to save the family as the British close in. His apparently suspicious actions ultimately prove protective.
- **Constance:** repeatedly asks players to discover her children's fates; grows increasingly distraught at each report. Learning that all four are dead triggers the **modern supernatural fire**.
- **Waitstill:** associated with signaling and communications.
- **Deliverance:** courier carrying Josiah's encrypted warning; dies **outside** the house.
- **Mindwell:** shy, hides, **listens/eavesdrops**; dies **inside** the house and witnesses **Remember's death**. Her testimony must convey apparent finality to Constance.
- **Remember (she):** **memorizes the ciphers**. She **does not write down or physically hold a codebook**. The network entrusts her with cipher knowledge, **not the messages themselves**. She dies **inside** the house. In the garage she supplies the missing decoding method, **not the explanation**: players themselves decode Josiah's warning and discover his protective intent.
- All four children are dead in the historical story. Their ghostly appearance is not evidence of physical survival.

## Historical trigger and tragedy — working narrative
The underlying disaster is the **parents' involvement in espionage** and the British investigation of the household. A neighbor may have reported suspicious signaling by Constance. Josiah learns the danger and dispatches Deliverance with an encrypted evacuation warning. Transmission takes time and is vulnerable to interception; compartmentalized ciphers protect the network but can prevent urgent information being used in time.

**Working chain, not all details locked:**
1. British soldiers arrive to investigate/arrest and search the Ashby household.
2. Waitstill is discovered signaling and is shot in a confrontation.
3. Deliverance, outside with Josiah's message, hides it in the backyard and is shot before he can bring it home.
4. The British search for the remaining children, who are hiding. The commanding officer orders a fire **to flush the hidden children out**. This is a specific, brutal tactical choice; the fire spreads disastrously.
5. Remember tries to escape and dies inside. Mindwell, concealed and listening, **witnesses Remember die**; she hides and also dies inside.
6. Mindwell's eavesdropping can reveal the soldiers' search and the officer's fire order, making the tragedy discoverable through play.

**User response:** the fire-to-flush-them-out scenario “feels good,” is sad in the desired way, and the user said “let's do it.” Treat it as the **accepted working version** for the next design session, while retaining the need to pressure-test physical staging and chronology.

**Important distinctions:** The *historical* fire follows soldiers' actions; the *modern* apparent fire follows **Constance's grief/rage**. They need not share an ignition mechanism. Constance's precise whereabouts, death, and historical knowledge are **not yet settled**. Whether Deliverance's warning was never delivered, was found too late, or could not be decoded remains open.

## Present-day encounter chain — committed framework / mechanics in development
1. **Waitstill — observation + logic.** At the spyglass, identify a particular yard signal among decoys and send the proper response. This establishes contact with Deliverance.
2. **Deliverance — hunt/search.** Players go to the backyard, retrace or search for his hidden parchment, physically recover Josiah's encrypted warning, and return to the séance table. The message has a **plausible misleading surface reading** if intercepted; players do not yet possess the key.
3. **Mindwell — hunt/search + listening + logic (proposed, not fully designed).** Players find listening points or overheard conversation fragments, reconstruct what soldiers searched for and the decision to use fire, locate Mindwell's hiding place, and learn that she witnessed Remember die. **The concrete apparatus, clue ordering, feedback, and how the player action unlocks her testimony remain open.** Do not assume a four-token chronological puzzle has been approved.
4. **Remember — codebreaking.** After Constance is told the last child's fate, her rage makes the house appear to burn, driving players to the garage. Remember communicates the **memorized cipher**. Players apply it to Deliverance's parchment and themselves infer Josiah was warning and protecting his family.
5. **Resolution.** The revelation may reconcile Constance, Josiah, and the four children, potentially releasing them; whether the players truly break a cycle or simply complete its reenactment remains deliberately ambiguous.

## Key narrative and game constraints
- **Do not treat the British as arbitrary enemies appearing solely to kill children.** Every action needs concrete motive, location, and causal sequence.
- Do not introduce physical cipher papers for Remember: **she is the cipher**.
- Mindwell must offer a **game**, not just a spoken account.
- Distinguish **historical order of deaths** from **player encounter order**; Remember dies before Mindwell can report witnessing her death, even though players meet Remember last.
- Keep the modern fire **before** the garage decoding.
- Return to the séance table after each major discovery, so Constance's escalating emotional response forms a repeating game rhythm.
- Proposed lines and props are brainstorming, not established dialogue or approved hardware.
