## 2. Continuing foundations

These are carried forward from Checkpoint 02 unless explicitly revised above.

### Story and play principles

- Design the historical events and present-day player actions together.
- Each child encounter must be an actual playable game grounded in what that character historically did.
- Encounter order remains **Waitstill → Deliverance → Mindwell → Remember**. Historical death order is a separate question.

## 9. Historical research retained for design use

These findings support the setting; they do not establish real historical events for the fictional Ashby family.

- **Regional origins:** Many New England walls accumulated as farmers moved stones from fields to their edges and existing fence lines. Forest clearing, erosion, and frost exposed stones in the glacial soils. Rough piles and irregular walls are historically plausible, not only neatly fitted masonry. [UConn — History in Ten Steps](https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/history-in-ten-steps/)
- **Timing:** UConn places most regional wall accumulation/construction roughly in **1750–1850**, with especially rapid construction around **1775–1825**. A Revolutionary-era wall is plausible, but not every surviving wall predates the Revolution. [UConn — Frequently Asked Questions](https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/frequently-asked-questions/)
- **Revolutionary use:** The National Park Service describes colonial fighters using stone walls as cover during the British retreat on **April 19, 1775**, and suggests many local walls were then relatively new. [NPS — Stone Walls of Minute Man](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/stone-walls-of-minute-man.htm)
- **Walls in modern woods:** Abandoned farms were reclaimed by forest; some walls survived, collapsed, or were partly removed for reuse. This supports a fictional fragment remaining in a now-wooded yard. [UConn — History in Ten Steps](https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/history-in-ten-steps/)
- **Training-field evidence:** A transcription of Westford's **1748 training-field deed** describes boundary landmarks including a maple growing out of a rock, a heap of stones, a marked black oak, and the corner of an existing wall. It is a useful model for a fictional property clue. [Westford — Training Field and Deed](https://www.westford.org/westford1775/Training_Field.html)

**Important correction:** The user recalled stone walls associated with cleared military training/parade grounds. The research supports the existence of colonial training fields and a deed referring to an existing wall. It does **not** establish that this wall was built to clear a military field, nor prove that it was not. The assistant initially overconfirmed the connection. Do not generalize military training as the origin of New England's stone walls or claim the user's particular remembered site has been identified.

No historical evidence was established that this particular kind of wall routinely served as a spy dead drop. That use is part of the fictional story.
