## 2. Continuing story and design principles

- Distinguish **the historical fire ordered during the soldiers’ search** from **Constance’s apparent supernatural fire in the present**.
- The final decoding must preserve the revelation that Josiah was trying to protect the family.

## 7. Mindwell’s special cup

### The user’s electronics proposal

The user suggested hiding electronics inside the cup, with an RFID tag or similar trigger at each listening location. Bringing the cup to a location would play the corresponding audio **inside the cup**, close to the listener’s ear.

A practical architecture to prototype is:

- Passive NFC tags at selected locations.
- An NFC reader and small controller in the cup.
- Locally stored recordings, an audio playback circuit, a battery, and a small speaker in the cup.
- Each tag identifies a location; the controller selects that location’s recording.
- Reader antenna toward the rim/surface; speaker toward the base/ear.

**This is a feasibility direction, not a chosen parts list or a tested design.** Short-range NFC suits deliberate placement at a spot; it does not imply room-wide automatic detection.

Compact audio modules exist. For example, the DFPlayer Mini is specified at 20 × 20 mm and supports stored audio with controller-directed playback and a speaker connection. That does not establish the size of the complete cup assembly. [DFRobot — DFPlayer Mini](https://www.dfrobot.com/product-1121.html)

NFC performance depends on antenna geometry and placement; nearby metal can reduce performance. The actual cup material, wall/door surface, battery placement, and antenna separation require testing. [NXP — NFC Antenna Design Made Easy](https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/smarter-world-blog/BL-NFC-ANTENNA-DESIGN-MADE-EASY)

Possible playback behavior: stable placement triggers a clip once; holding the cup there does not continually restart it; lifting and replacing permits replay. This behavior remains a proposal.

### Retained landscape research

These findings support a plausible setting; they do not establish actual Ashby events.

- Many New England stone walls developed as farmers moved stones from fields to boundaries and fences. Rough piles and fragments are plausible. [UConn — History in Ten Steps](https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/history-in-ten-steps/)
- Much wall accumulation dates broadly to 1750–1850, with rapid construction around 1775–1825. A Revolutionary-era wall is possible; not every surviving wall is that old. [UConn — Frequently Asked Questions](https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/frequently-asked-questions/)

- A transcription of Westford’s 1748 training-field deed uses trees, stones, and an existing wall as boundary landmarks. It is useful inspiration for the property clue. [Westford — Training Field and Deed](https://www.westford.org/westford1775/Training_Field.html)
