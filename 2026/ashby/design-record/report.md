## 1. The story as it stands

This is what participants see, hear, and are told, in the order they meet it. No mechanics, measurements, or build notes appear here; part 3 holds those in the same order. The thread follows the accepted working sequence of CP05 §3 as carried into CP06 §3, with CP06's "Critical correction" governing Mindwell's scene. Each scene opens with a status line. The prose after it is the experience.

### Scene 1. The séance begins

Status: Working [CP05 §3, CP06 §3]. Constance's object is Open; the locket is a liked provisional example [CP05 §4].

The game is played downstairs, at and around a séance table in the main room. Tamari sits at the table as the spiritualist. She knows only what the public knows: a colonial family lived on this property, and there was a terrible fire.

She holds one object that belonged to Constance Ashby, the mother of the house. The working example is a locket Josiah gave her, possibly holding a braid of their hair. Through it Tamari makes first contact, and Constance speaks. She introduces her four missing children. Her husband Josiah is away. A message from him is expected: Deliverance is carrying it, and Waitstill is keeping watch for him. Constance does not know what became of her children. She wants two things from the participants. Find out what happened to the children. Find out what the message said.

She gives the participants a reason to look for Waitstill's belongings, or for the place he kept watch.

### Scene 2. Waitstill at the window

Status: Working [CP05 §3, CP06 §3]. The reply "the coast is clear" is Committed in substance [CP03 §1, CP05 §1]. The number of lights and decoys is Open.

The participants find Waitstill's spyglass themselves. Brought to Tamari, it connects her to Waitstill keeping watch, and he supplies what they need to take his place at the window. They look out through the spyglass into the dark yard. Out there are lights, more than one. Only one is Deliverance. The participants pick out the right signal and answer it with the agreed sign: the coast is clear.

Deliverance's lights answer back. His reply is a sequence, and the sequence opens a chest. Inside the chest is a map.

### Scene 3. The map, and the first death

Status: Working [CP05 §1 and §3, CP06 §1 and §3]. This timing supersedes CP03 and CP04, which revealed Waitstill's death when the letter came back.

The participants bring the map to the table because they need to know what it shows. It was Waitstill's, something he kept or used in the family's secret work. Through it Tamari reaches Waitstill's last errand. He went out to collect the message from the hiding place he knew. British soldiers caught him and killed him. He never came back inside.

Tamari returns to Constance, and the participants tell her what they learned. This is the first death reported to her. She did not know.

The map opens the next layer of the family's story. There is a secret message network. Deliverance carried messages. Waitstill watched for them and collected them. Constance's own part in that work can begin to show here. She recognizes the old property on the map and explains the stone wall and the hiding place in it. The participants' task is now plain: use the map, find the hiding place, bring back the message.

### Scene 4. Into the backyard

Status: Working [CP03 §4 and §5, CP05 §3 and §6, CP06 §3 and §6]. A pursuit effect behind the participants is Proposed [CP03 §8]. A memorial marker for Deliverance is Proposed and Open [CP03 §5].

The participants go out through the office door into the real backyard at night. The map describes an old property: a great tree, a recognizable boulder, a wall. The yard has many trees, and a living one may draw them first. Then they see it. Beside the right boulder is a stump. The tree in the old description is gone. The hiding place is in the wall fragment next to it.

They recover a letter on vellum from the wall and bring it inside. If the pursuit effect is used, the yard feels unsafe behind them, as if something is following. Nothing chases them.

### Scene 5. The letter, and the second death

Status: Working [CP05 §3, CP06 §3, CP06 Latest "Critical correction" item 2]. The letter's surface content is Working; its wording is Open [CP03 §8].

Back at the table a participant reads the letter aloud so everyone hears the same words. It is a dull domestic note from Josiah. Business will keep him late. Here are instructions for the household. And a small request about supper, something he does not often ask for.

Tamari contacts Deliverance through the letter he carried and hid. He tells his part. He brought Josiah's message toward the house. He realized he was being followed. He left the letter at the agreed hiding place and kept moving so the soldiers would follow him and not find it. They caught him and killed him outside.

The participants tell Constance. Both of her sons are dead. And the letter they went out into the dark to recover says nothing. After everything, it is about supper. Her hurt deepens into a sense that Josiah abandoned them.

By the end of this scene the participants know both boys are dead and that soldiers were on their way to the house. They suspect the letter means more than it says. They cannot yet read it.

### Scene 6. Looking for Mindwell

Status: Working [CP05 §3, CP06 §3]. The five-cup display and the royal cup are Committed [CP06 Latest, CAPT]. Constance's exact clue is deferred by the captain's choice [CP06 Latest].

Constance still needs to know what happened to her daughters. She describes Mindwell: quiet and shy, but a habitual eavesdropper who listened at doors for secrets, and who always used one particular cup, the one she inherited from her grandmother.

On a shelf downstairs sit five decorated cups among some plates, ordinary household ceramics. One shows a sailing ship. One a blue flowering branch. One an ornate crest with initials. One reads "Wilkes and Liberty No. 45", an odd thing to find in a magistrate's house. And one bears the royal cipher GR, for King George. Constance's memory of her family tells the participants which one was the grandmother's. They take it down.

### Scene 7. Mindwell listens

Status: Working framework with the mechanism Open [CP06 §3 and §7]. The three required outcomes are Committed by the captain's correction [CP06 Latest "Critical correction" items 1, 3, 4]. What participants hear at each spot is Open. The hiding place under the stairs is Working [CP05 §9].

The cup connects Tamari to Mindwell. Mindwell's memory begins before she knows anything is wrong. Waitstill went out to the hiding place and should have been back by now. He is not. She is frightened, and she does what she always does. She takes her cup to a door and listens.

The participants take the cup to the places Mindwell listened and hear what she heard. Soldiers are in the house, searching for the family's hidden children. An officer gives an order: set a fire and drive them out. The fire spreads. Mindwell is hidden in a small pocket under the stairs, hearing more than she can see: sounds, shadows, movement at ankle height through a cracked door. She hears and glimpses enough to know that her sister Remember tried to escape and died inside the house. Mindwell stays concealed and dies there too.

Mindwell also gives the participants something about the letter. She knows the family well enough to know that Josiah's supper request is not like him. What she knows moves the letter forward one step. It does not unlock it.

The participants come away knowing that Remember is dead, and enough about Mindwell's own end to tell her mother.

### Scene 8. Constance's fire

Status: Committed [CP02 §Physical staging, carried to CP06 §3].

The participants return to the table and tell Constance the last of it. All four of her children are dead. Her grief turns to rage, and the house appears to catch fire around the participants. They have to leave the room. The way out is the door to the garage.

### Scene 9. Remember, in the garage

Status: Committed in outcome [CP02 §Characters, carried to CP06 §3]. How Remember appears is Open [CP05 §8]. The cipher is Open. The two payoffs named below are Working and Open respectively.

In the garage the participants meet Remember. She carried no codebook and no messages. She carried the ciphers in her head. She gives the participants the method and nothing more. They apply it themselves to the letter they recovered.

The dull note about supper was never about supper. Hidden inside it is Josiah's warning to his family, sent to get them to safety as the British closed in. He was not late. He was not indifferent. He was trying to save them, and the message never reached them in time.

Whether this reconciles Constance with Josiah and the children, and whether it releases them, is left open. Whether the participants changed anything, or only replayed a night that already happened, is meant to stay ambiguous. The Wilkes cup on the shelf may turn out to have been Josiah's, a quiet sign of where his sympathies lay. The locket Constance began with may mean something different now.

### 2.3 The historical night

The trigger is the parents' involvement in espionage, which brings a British investigation of the household [Working, CP02 §Historical trigger]. A neighbor may have reported suspicious signaling by Constance [Working, CP02 §Historical trigger].

Outdoor sequence [Committed causal outline, CP03 §3, carried unchanged to CP06 §5]:

1. Josiah sends Deliverance with the concealed warning.
2. Deliverance approaches the Ashby property. Waitstill watches and signals that the coast is clear. In normal operations this exchange supports collection at the agreed dead drop.
3. Deliverance realizes he is being followed by the British. The timing relative to the signal is Open.
4. He deposits the warning at the drop and keeps moving so the pursuers do not find it.
5. The British catch and kill Deliverance away from the drop.
6. Waitstill goes out to retrieve the warning from the place he already knows. He is caught and killed and never returns.

Waitstill knew where to go. There was no location puzzle for him. The participants' puzzle recovers knowledge he already had [Committed distinction, CP03 §3].

Indoor sequence [Working, CP02 §Historical trigger, carried to CP06 §5]:

1. Mindwell expects Waitstill back. He does not come. She is frightened and listens for news or danger [Committed motivation, CP03 §1 and §7].
2. If the bird call is kept, Waitstill gives a private call that Mindwell knows as a warning, and it prompts her to hide. It does not tell her he is dead [Working option, CP03 §7].
3. British soldiers search the household because of the family's espionage. They search for the hidden children.
4. An officer orders a fire to flush the hidden children out. It spreads disastrously.
5. Remember attempts to escape and dies inside.
6. Mindwell, concealed and listening, learns enough of Remember's death to report it, and dies inside.

The captain accepted the fire-to-flush-them-out version in CP02 as the working version, saying it "feels good" and "let's do it", with the standing requirement that the soldiers' motives, positions, and actions be made concrete and convincing, including why they do not rescue the children [CP02 §Outstanding work, CP03 §7].

Gaps that the checkpoints say must not be filled by assumption [CP03 §3, CP05 §5, CP06 §5]:

- Whether Waitstill reaches the cache or touches the letter.
- Why the letter remains at the drop if he ever removes it.
- How Waitstill's all-clear viewpoint fits Deliverance being pursued.
- The relative timing of the two outdoor deaths and the search inside.
- Where each group of soldiers is, what they see, and why they act as they do.
- Why the chest stores a map and why Deliverance's signal supplies its combination.
- How a map carries Waitstill's retrieval experience without inventing a navigation problem for him.
- How limited, fragmentary observation gives Mindwell enough certainty to report Remember's death.

Two fires [Committed, CP02 §Important distinctions]. The historical fire follows the soldiers' order. The modern apparent fire follows Constance's grief and rage. They need not share an ignition mechanism.

### 2.5 Research retained for design use

None of this establishes real Ashby events. It supports the setting.

Stone walls [CP03 §9, carried to CP06 §10]:

- Many New England walls accumulated as farmers moved field stones to boundaries and fence lines. Rough piles and irregular fragments are plausible. UConn, History in Ten Steps: https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/history-in-ten-steps/
- Most wall accumulation dates to about 1750 to 1850, with rapid construction around 1775 to 1825. A Revolutionary-era wall is plausible; not every surviving wall is that old. UConn FAQ: https://stonewall.uconn.edu/resources/primer/frequently-asked-questions/
- Colonial fighters used stone walls as cover during the British retreat on April 19, 1775. NPS: https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/stone-walls-of-minute-man.htm
- Westford's 1748 training-field deed names a maple growing out of a rock, a heap of stones, a marked black oak, and the corner of an existing wall as boundary landmarks. A model for the fictional property clue. https://www.westford.org/westford1775/Training_Field.html
- Correction to preserve: the research does not show that wall was built to clear a military field, and the captain's remembered site was not identified. The assistant overconfirmed this once. Using a wall as a spy dead drop is a story choice, not a documented practice.

Cups [CP06 §12, CAPT]:

- Royal GR ceramics: a Colonial Williamsburg stoneware tankard dated 1725 to 1750 with a blue GR medallion; a related entry explains George Rex. https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/25510/tankard and https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/17972/jug
- Creamware reference chosen by the captain: the Museum of the American Revolution's "Success to Boston" mug, eighteenth-century English creamware. https://www.amrevmuseum.org/collection/success-to-boston-mug [CAPT]
- Ship decoration: a porcelain shipping mug, about 1750, with a ship and initialed cartouche. https://www.christies.com/lot/lot-6187702
- Floral decoration: a Worcester porcelain mug dated 1755 to 1760 in the blue Prunus Fence pattern. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1928-1113-1-CR
- Crest and initials: a porcelain coffee cup dated 1760 to 1770 with heraldic decoration and the initials H.F.L. Supports the motif only; no Ashby arms or initials are assigned. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_Franks-789-
- Wilkes and Liberty: the museum's 1763 to 1765 creamware mug. https://www.amrevmuseum.org/collection/wilkes-and-liberty-no-45-mug
- A royal commemorative Worcester mug of 1761 with Queen Charlotte's portrait is supporting history, not a selected cup. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1887-0307-V-28
- The museum shop's modern Liberty mug and George Washington Standard mug are not documented reproductions. A field-of-stars cup was not selected [Discarded, CP06 §12].

Storytelling tools [CP06 §12]: Ink and Inform 7 were discussed. No tool was adopted. The retained principle is to track separately what happened, what each character knows, and what the participants have discovered.

## 4. Design principles and rules

Standing rules, grouped. Each carries its status and the latest source that states it.

### 4.1 Story and play

- Design the historical events and the present-day participant actions together, not as separate timelines patched together [Committed, CP02 §Core design principles, last seen CP04 §2].
- Four child encounters in this order: Waitstill, Deliverance, Mindwell, Remember. Historical death order is a separate question [Committed, CP02, CP06 §2].
- Every encounter is a playable game grounded in what that child did: watching and signaling, carrying and retrieving, listening for secrets, remembering codes [Committed, CP02, CP06 §2].
- Four puzzle families: logic, observation, hunt/search, codebreaking. Hybrids welcome [Committed, CP02, last seen CP04 §2].
- Puzzles must be easier than their creators first think. One principal realization per small puzzle. Each encounter teaches a pattern participants can reuse [Committed, CP02, CP03 §2 reinforced, CP06 §2].
- Participants have real agency: they solve, interpret, and report. Tamari facilitates contact and does not perform their deductions [Committed, CP05 §2].
- Give participants a clear reason to pursue each lead. "Find her hiding place" is insufficient unless they know why it advances their goal [Committed, CP05 §2].
- Discoveries yield practical progress and lore together [Committed, CP05 §2].
- A little misdirection is wanted: an obvious thing to try that can be wrong, then the correction. The wrong turn is an invitation, never a mandatory failure [Committed intent, CP03 §5].
- The British are not arbitrary enemies who appear only to kill children. Every action needs concrete motive, location, and causal sequence [Committed, CP02 §Key narrative constraints, last seen CP04 §7].
- Remember is the cipher. No physical codebook, ever [Committed, CP02, CP06 §8].
- Mindwell must offer a game, not a spoken account, and her account stays fragmentary and ambiguous while still establishing Remember's death [Committed, CP02, CP05 §1, CP06 Latest].
- The modern fire comes before the garage decoding. The historical fire and the modern fire are distinct events with different causes [Committed, CP02].
- Do not reveal the full warning early to explain away Constance's anger [Committed, CP03 §8, CP06 §8].

### 4.2 Supernatural model

- Present-day participant actions uncover moments from the past, and those discoveries provoke present-day reactions from the ghosts [Committed working model, CP03 §8, CP06 §2].
- The metaphysics stay unsettled: spirits, place-bound recordings, memories, or something between. Whether participants alter the haunting or reenact it stays ambiguous [Committed, CP02, CP06 §2].
- Supernatural transitions need concrete, achievable cues. "Audio memory" or "hearing memories" is not a specification; say what plays, where, what triggers it, and what participants learn [Committed, CP03 §8, CP06 §7].
- An outdoor pursuit may be an echo of danger without a historical soldier chasing anyone [Accepted possibility, CP03 §8].

### 4.3 Object-contact model

- A personal object is the focus for Tamari's contact with its associated person [Committed working version, CP05 §1 and §4].
- More than one object can connect to one person: spyglass to Waitstill keeping watch, map to Waitstill's retrieval, letter to Deliverance, cup to Mindwell [Working, CP05 §4].
- Contacts can be voices or fragments of experience. Whether they are conscious spirits answering questions is not fixed [CP05 §4].
- Tamari starts with one artifact, Constance's. The spyglass, map, letter, and cup are discoveries, not items she hands out [Committed, CP05 §4].
- Constance's contact is distinct from the children's so participants always have something to tell her [CP05 §4].
- Limit what each contact reveals. An early object must not give away what later discoveries are meant to earn [CP05 §4].
- A proposed rule that Tamari's touch opens an environmental memory is not approved [CP06 §7].

### 4.4 Séance rhythm and knowledge discipline

- Each stage has motivation, a lead-in, a participant action, a discovery, and a meaningful return to the séance. Lore grows with the returns. A plot recap is not an encounter [Committed, CP05 §1].
- Return to the séance table after each major discovery so Constance's escalating response is the game's rhythm [Committed, CP02].
- Constance does not know her children are dead. Her exposition cannot supply the answers she asks for. She recalls what she knew in life and cannot narrate the raid [Committed, CP05 §1, CP06 §3].
- Track separately what happened, what each character knows, and what the participants have discovered [Retained principle, CP06 §12]. In particular, participants know Waitstill is dead when Mindwell's memory begins; Mindwell does not [Committed, CP03 §7, CP06 §5].
- Proposed lines and props are brainstorming, not established dialogue or approved hardware, until marked otherwise [Committed, CP02].

### 4.5 Staging

- Main play downstairs, séance table as hub; the real backyard for the letter; the garage for the finale [Committed, CP02, CP06 §2].
- Lighting cues direct attention to activity areas, which need not be separate rooms [Committed, CP02, last seen CP04 §2].
- Mindwell's cup is nonmetal [Committed, CP06 Latest, CAPT].
- No real fire. Safe theatrical effects and clear evacuation paths [Committed, CP02].
- Whether the project happens at all depends on doing it cheaply with very little preparation [Committed constraint, DICT].
- Nothing physical has been built, bought, recorded, or tested as of CP06. The listening-cup build guide is the first build work under way [CP06 §13, CAPT].

### 4.6 Working method with the design partner

- Walkthroughs cover actual participant actions, information, feedback, and transitions, never another plot recap. When asked to walk it through, the partner performs the walkthrough [Committed, CP03 §6, CP06 Latest].
- Do the connective design work; do not ask the captain to supply the missing link. Do not invent a premise and present it as already discussed. Do not propose a pause unless asked. Historical research is not needed to reason from established story facts [Committed, CP06 Latest].
- Keep outdoor mechanics parked unless a continuity issue requires them [Committed, CP03 §10, CP06 §6].
- Do not restart cup selection, stone-wall brainstorming, or ear-horn research [CP06 Resume, CP03 header, CP05 §7].
- Do not write Constance's dialogue yet; the captain parked that task [CP06 Latest].
- For walk-through designs, keep the story first and the mechanics apart [captain's standing rule, this task's instructions].
