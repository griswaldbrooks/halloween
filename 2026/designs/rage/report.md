# Halloween 2026: the Rage ending, complete design

Task `hw-design-rage`, 2026-10-09. One of two sibling designs the captain asked for ("one with a resurrection ending and one with the rage ending"). This document is the rage game, built against the synthesis principles (`data/hw-synthesis/report.md` section 0), the handoff's story, and the rage playtest's PROPOSAL, refined rather than replaced. Everything that is taste, story darkness, cost, or a choice the handoff left open is marked **CAPTAIN CALL** with a default; the design runs on the defaults. Section 12 collects them. Section 13 is the simulation that proves it runs. Nothing in the halloween repo changed; the engine copy with my changes is under `data/hw-design-rage/sim/`.

Conventions: times are m:ss. "The medium" is Tamari, live. "The mother" is a recorded voice (call 11). "The captain" runs effects from outside the room. Four players are named by the child they are bound to: the Deliverance player, the Waitstill player, and so on.

---

## 1. One page

**Pitch.** Four guests sit down to a séance in a house that burned on All Hallows' Eve 1777. The medium knows the folklore: a magistrate's wife and four children lost in a fire while he was away. The mother's ghost knows less than that. She believes her children got out, and she cannot leave the table to look. The guests go out into the house for her, one child's room at a time, and bring back what each child left. Each thing they bring narrows what she can believe. The fourth proves all four died inside the house, and she turns: she finds her husband's seal on a paper in her son's satchel, decides his oath killed them, and sets about putting him into the fire. The guests, each now missing the sense their child used, have the only thing that can stop her: the report her children never delivered, in four parts, in the form she taught them, said aloud by one voice.

**The objective sentence** (the medium, at the turn, with the sound lowered):

> "She means to put him into the fire. Before it reaches him, give her the report her children never gave: what was seen, what was heard, what was carried, what was said, and one of you say it to her whole. Each child she hears will fall quiet. You'll know it worked when the screaming stops and she answers him. Start with what is in front of you."

**The three finale sentences** (the playtest's PROPOSAL, kept):

- She now intends to put her husband into the fire: to condemn him as he condemned her, and let the haunting burn him as it burned the children.
- If the players fail, the fire reaches him: his voice joins the children's screams, then the soundscape cuts to silence, the lights drop, and the medium closes the circle naming who is at rest and who is not.
- The players win when they assemble the four parts of his reason from the four relics on the table, in the family's report form, and one of them says it aloud to her before the fire reaches him.

**Run length.** 25:00 is the design target for a group that needs one or two hints; 29:30 is the worst case with every bypass used and the clock run out. The fire clock is 7:00 and starts after the fourth curse (call 4). Section 9 has the table.

**Cast and crew on the night.**

| Who | Where | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Tamari, the medium | In the room with the group, always | Frames, binds, hints, reads the table, confirms every cue, speaks the objective, closes the circle |
| The captain | Outside the room, console with a view (camera or sightline) | Raps and lantern (the spirit's yes/no), the mother's lines, relic cues, the turn, the fire timeline and stems, the tier ending, the real stop |
| Recorded voices | Speakers | The mother; the father; the officer; four children (one short beat each) |
| Optional second pair of hands | Hallway | Resets rooms while the medium walks a group out; greets the next group |

**Rooms** (propose; **CAPTAIN CALL 12**, an afternoon's walk decides):

| Role | Room | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hub, the séance | Living room | Biggest table, most seats, the fire effect has walls to throw shadows on, the front door is the exit |
| Deliverance, the courier | Kitchen | The back door the courier used; a hearth or a wall for the hearth flat |
| Waitstill, the lookout | The room with the best street window (dining room or front bedroom) | A real window and a real road |
| Mindwell, the listener | Hall closet, pantry, or under-stair cupboard sharing a wall with the living room | She listened through the wall to her father's study; cramped is right |
| Remember, the memorizer | Small bedroom | The nursery |

Constraints on the choice: every spoke within fifteen seconds' walk of the table, no stairs if the Waitstill player's seat can be avoided on an upper floor (the curses happen only at the table, so stairs are a pacing cost, not a safety one), and the hub's door to the outside stays unlocked and clear for the whole run.

**What the folklore says** (what the medium believes at the start): Ashby House burned on All Hallows' Eve 1777 while the magistrate was away at the sessions. His wife and four children died in the fire. Her ghost walks the house calling the children's names. The town says she set the fire herself, in grief or madness, because the magistrate never came home again.

**What is true** (what the players assemble; the handoff's section 3 with the blanks filled):

1. Constance ran a Patriot ring out of a Loyalist magistrate's house. Her four children were the ring.
2. Tillery saw the black petticoat on the line and told the officer of the garrison.
3. On the morning of 31 October the officer came to the house. Mindwell, under the study floor, heard him tell Josiah: swear to her treason and your children are not charged; refuse and they hang beside her.
4. Josiah rode to town with the officer at six bells by the town road. Waitstill logged it. Before he left he told Remember: whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four; tell your mother.
5. Constance was at market in town. Josiah swore before the court. She was arrested there.
6. The officer wrote the protection he had promised: the children of Josiah Ashby are to be spared, their father having given sworn evidence. Deliverance, who had come to town with his mother as her courier, was sent home with it.
7. A detachment under different orders had already been sent to the house to seize the papers. They came by the Landing road at twelve bells. Waitstill logged it and showed BLACK, ONE, BLACK. Deliverance arrived first and barred the back door. The soldiers sealed the house and fired it. Mindwell was under the stair; Remember under her bed; Waitstill at the window; Deliverance at the barred door with the protection in the drop.
8. Constance was hanged at the Landing two days later. Josiah was made to witness it. He did not know the children were dead until he rode home to ash. He never knew that she died believing he had simply sold her.

**How dark the spokes and the fire are (CAPTAIN CALL 7).** Default: each child's death is implied by where they were and stated by the medium in one plain sentence at the return, never shown. The fire soundscape's screams are wordless, distant, and layered, one voice per child, never a child saying words while burning. The children's recorded beats are each a few seconds of a child doing their job, cut before the end.

## 3. The opening, scripted

Budget 3:00. Two parts: thirty seconds out of character at the door, then in character at the table. Figure: the hub table, seen from above, is a square with the medium at the north side; the four players sit east, south, west and at the medium's left, with the Waitstill player's seat the one with a wall behind it and the Deliverance player beside her (he is her guide later). On the table: four painted outlines (satchel, slate, small book, board) at the four seats, a battery lantern in the centre, a slate and chalk, four objects at four seats, the name-board (a plank with the four children's names).

### 3.1 At the door, out of character (0:30)

Tamari, as herself, before anyone enters:

> "Thirty seconds of real talk, then I'm someone else. This is a séance game; it gets dark and loud for the last ten minutes. One of you will wear a blindfold, sitting down, with a friend's hand on your arm. One of you will wear earmuffs. One of you wears big gloves. One of you can't talk. Nothing goes in your mouth, nobody is tied, nothing is locked. If you want out of any of it, take it off; the game keeps going and you lose nothing. The word is **Cut**. Anyone says Cut, or stop, lights come up and everything stops. If it's just too much, say **Less** and I'll turn it down. Who's happy to be the one who can't see for a while? Who's fine with earmuffs? Who's the writer who can keep quiet? And who's clumsy?"

She assigns by consent (call 14): the blindfold to someone comfortable sitting still, the muffs to a careful reader, the silence to a confident writer, the gloves to the talker. She writes nothing down; she remembers four faces.

### 3.2 The framing (0:45)

In character, lights low, the lantern lit. The medium seats them, names the house and the night, and tells the folklore version in six sentences (section 2, "what the folklore says"). Then the contract, which is the objective of the first half:

> "She is still here. She is bound to this table; she can't go where her children went. You can. She's asking us to find what became of them. Go together, one room at a time, and bring back whatever each child left. We'll put it here, where they sat, and ask her together."

### 3.3 Binding and the objects (0:45)

The medium places one object at each seat and names the child as she does it. The bound guest takes it up.

| Seat | Object | What it is later |
|---|---|---|
| Deliverance | A pair of oversized leather riding gauntlets | The gloves curse |
| Waitstill | A black lace mourning veil with a sleep mask sewn inside the fall | The blindfold, applied by lowering the veil |
| Mindwell | Passive over-ear hearing protectors, period-dressed in black cloth | The muffling (call 23: default real muffs) |
| Remember | A black ribbon, tied at the wrist, and the slate and chalk | The silence; the writing channel |

Then the one rule, plainly, and the demonstration:

> "One rule of the circle. When she calls your child's name, do the one thing your object is for, and keep helping with everything you have left. Take it off the moment you want to. Watch: **Deliverance.**"

The Deliverance player puts on the gauntlets. The medium: "Pick up the chalk." He can't. "Remember, pick it up for him." She does. "That's all it ever is. Gloves off." Fifteen seconds; the rule has been used once, with the object, and its substitute shown. Then the writing channel: "The dead read better than they hear. Remember, write your child's name and show it round instead of saying it." She writes REMEMBER and shows it. The circle has now used the slate once.

### 3.4 The spirit's voice and the tutorial gate (0:45)

> "She answers plainly if you ask plainly. Once for yes." The captain raps once under the table and the lantern flickers once. "Twice for no." Two raps, two flickers. "You'll see it as well as hear it. Remember: write the four names on the slate, and ask her which to find first. Point to each."

The Remember player writes the four names and points. At DELIVERANCE the captain raps once and the lantern flickers once. This is the hint channel (section 4, channel 2) used by doing: a question asked through the slate, answered on sound and light. The medium turns it into the send-off, with the reason:

> "Deliverance. The courier. He went out the back, she says, the night of the fire. The kitchen. Stay together; she can't follow you off this table and neither can I hint from here. Bring back what he left, and we'll put it where he sat."

The medium goes with them. Time: 3:00 from the door.

**What the opening has taught by doing:** the objective and the return point (3.2); each object, applied and removed, and the substitute (3.3); the slate as a channel (3.3, 3.4); the spirit's yes and no on sound and light together (3.4); stay together; the first door, for a reason.

## 4. The hint channel

Defined once; every room's hint lines in section 5 use it. The medium's character does not know the history. She does know how the circle works, and the actor knows every puzzle cold (principle 36).

**Three channels, one command.**

1. **Attention, by the medium's craft.** She reads the table, never the puzzle: where the spirit is looking, what went cold. "She keeps turning to the hearth." Names where to look, never what to do.
2. **The spirit's yes and no.** A rap under the table and a lantern flicker, one for yes, two for no, fired by the captain. The medium asks a leading question aloud on the group's behalf. Visible, so the muffled player gets it later; audible, so the blind player gets it.
3. **The spirit calls you.** The command channel from the opening's rule: "She calls Waitstill: your hand on the kettle's iron." A named player does a named thing. It is the bypass; it costs nothing and carries the evidence forward (immersive failure table).

The optional face-down card per child (escape report 4.5 channel 3) is **CAPTAIN CALL 24**, default out: at a 3:15 spoke with the medium in the room, three channels are enough and a fourth is a rule to teach.

**The ladder, by time stuck in a room** (escape report 4.4, scaled; "stuck" means no visible progress toward the gate, judged by the medium, not by effort on a prop):

| Time stuck | Tier | Channel | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 1:30 | Watch | 1 | She watches who is idle and hands them a job in character ("Mindwell, she wants you at the window") |
| 1:30 | Nudge | 1 | One sentence naming where to look |
| 3:00 | Push | 2 | A yes/no question that names the mechanism |
| 4:00 | Bypass | 3 | "She calls [child]: do [the step]." The room is done inside its slot; the evidence goes home |

No cap on hints (principle 15). Hints are offered, never waited for (principle 14). A hint arrives only after the group has had thirty seconds since its last visible progress, so no breakthrough is taken away (principle 16).

**In the finale**, the same ladder at half the times (0:45 nudge, 1:30 push, 2:00 bypass per piece), and each channel has a route through every curse:

| Player | Nudge and push reach them by | Bypass reaches them by |
|---|---|---|
| Gloved | Voice | Voice |
| Blind | Voice; the raps | Voice |
| Muffled | The lantern flicker; the medium writes one word on the slate and holds it up | The medium writes the command on the slate and points |
| Silenced | Voice; she answers by pointing or writing | Voice |

The medium repeats any instruction in plain words on request at any time; confusion about a rule is never a puzzle (immersive failure table).

## 5. Four rooms, one per child

Order is fixed: Deliverance, Waitstill, Mindwell, Remember. The spirit names each door at the previous return (section 5.5), so the players never choose. The first room teaches slowly (escape report option B). Each room has one gate, all four players busy at once, the information in one place and the thing to work in another, and plants in its bound player what that player will need at the table with a faculty gone. Each section stands alone for a builder.

Every room shares: a hidden speaker and a small trigger board (the captain's usual Arduino and an MP3 module), one bin for its props, no locks, no open flame, nothing destroyable, nothing hidden that must be re-hidden, and a single piece of evidence that is the relic.

### 5.1 Room 1: Deliverance, the courier. The kitchen.

**Time budget 4:00** (walk 0:15, read the room 0:20, satchel and card 0:40, work out and do the drop 1:10, cavity and the medium's line 0:30, walk back 0:15, place and return beat 0:50). Sim estimate for the gate: 90 s of effort.

**Why it is in the story.** Deliverance was the courier. The drop was where a message waited when the courier could not hand it over. His last act was to use it.

**What the players see on entering.** A kitchen, mostly bare. The back door (a real door kept shut, or a door flat if the real back door is the house's exit; see section 10) has a paper pasted across the jamb, printed in a period hand: "SEALED by order of the Crown. Nothing to be removed. 1 November 1777." A courier's satchel hangs on a peg beside it. On the hearth shelf, a boy's cap. The hearth is a brick face about 1.6 m wide and 0.9 m tall (the house's own hearth dressed with a plywood face, or a freestanding flat against a wall). An iron rod sticks out of the brick at the far left near the floor, where an andiron's leg would be; a kettle hangs from a crane whose iron bar comes out of the brick at the far right at chest height. Nothing else in the room invites touching.

Figure (front view of the hearth face): a rectangle 1.6 × 0.9 m painted as brick. Bottom-left corner, 0.3 m up: a short horizontal rod with a ring end, labelled "andiron's iron". Top-right, 1.1 m up: a longer rod with the kettle hanging from it, labelled "kettle's iron". Dead centre, 0.75 m up: one brick drawn with a dotted outline, labelled "the brick behind the kettle (hinged)". Arrows show the two rods pulled outward 25 mm and the centre brick pushed inward.

**The puzzle, exactly.**

The satchel has two buckles. Inside is one folded square of paper in the ring's fold: four flaps, one marked with a small ink arrow. Opening it any way but arrow-first then clockwise is merely awkward (the flaps tuck; nothing tears). Unfolded, it is the route card in the mother's hand:

> D.
> If they come while I am from home: bar the door, and put what you carry in the drop.
> The drop is the hearth. It takes three. One hand on the kettle's iron, one on the andiron's. Pull both and hold. The third pushes the brick behind the kettle.
> Then run for the Landing. Burn this.

Solution logic: two players pull the two irons outward at the same moment and hold them (they are 1.6 m apart, one at the floor and one at chest height, so no single person can hold both and push); a third player pushes the brick behind the kettle; it swings inward on its top hinge. If either iron is released the bolts spring back and the brick will not move. The fourth player reads the card and calls the moment ("both, now, hold"). Nothing in the card is a code; the two irons and the brick are named by the fixed objects on the hearth.

**Four players at once.** Reader and caller (holds the card, directs); low iron (crouching); high iron (standing); pusher. The reader is the one job the others cannot do while holding.

**What physically happens on solve.** The brick drops inward with a wooden clack. Behind it is a cavity 20 × 15 × 10 cm. In it: one square of paper in the ring's fold, sealed shut with red wax stamped **JA**. Nothing else. The medium, who has followed the group in, reads the room aloud in her own perception, which is also the fate: "He came home. He put it in the drop, as she told him. And the door is sealed from the outside. He didn't run." The relic is the satchel with the sealed paper inside it. The medium: "Leave his seal shut. Bring it to her."

**The relic.** The satchel, with the sealed paper in it. Its outline is painted on the table at the Deliverance seat.

**The reveal.** Role: the courier (the card, the satchel). Fate, partial: he came home and was still inside when the house was sealed. The mother's recorded reaction at the return (5.5) refuses it.

**What it plants in its bearer.** The Deliverance player has unfolded the ring's fold with his own hands. In the finale, gloved, he will direct another's hands through the same fold on the sealed paper.

**In-character hint lines.**

| Tier | Line |
|---|---|
| Watch | "Mindwell, she wants your hands on something. Waitstill, look low." |
| Nudge 1:30 | "She keeps turning to the hearth. Her boy's hands were quick, she says, and there were three of them." |
| Push 3:00 | "Is it the irons? [one rap] Both of them? [one rap] At once? [one rap]" |
| Bypass 4:00 | "She calls Waitstill: your hand on the kettle's iron. Remember: the andiron. Mindwell: push the brick behind the kettle. Deliverance, call it. Now." |

**Reset procedure (0:20).** Pull both irons, swing the brick closed, release; the bolts hold it. Put a fresh sealed paper in the cavity (make six in advance; each is the same printed text in the ring's fold with a wax seal). Refold the route card arrow-first and put it in the satchel. Hang the satchel. Cap on the shelf.

### 5.2 Room 2: Waitstill, the lookout. The street window.

**Time budget 3:15** (walk 0:15, read the room 0:20, key and last row 1:00, standing orders and signal 0:40, fate beat and medium's line 0:20, walk back 0:15, place and return beat 0:25). Sim estimate for the gate: 75 s.

**Why it is in the story.** Waitstill kept the window. She logged the roads on her slate in her own marks and showed the ring's signals with the lantern. Her last act was the warn-off.

**What the players see on entering.** A chair at the window. On a stand aimed through the glass, a spyglass. On the sill, a tin lantern with a sliding shutter; its candle (an LED) is lit. Painted on the wall beside the window, at reading height for someone standing at the glass, the standing orders. On the opposite wall by the door, hanging on a nail, a hand slate 30 × 20 cm covered in rows of small marks in white paint. Between them on the wall, a child's painted map: the house in the middle, a road running up the wall to a steeple labelled TOWN, a road running down to a jetty labelled THE LANDING, a square field labelled TILLERY, woods.

Figure (plan view): the door at the bottom-left with the slate beside it; the window at the top-right with the chair, the spyglass stand and the lantern on the sill; the standing orders painted just left of the window; the map on the long wall between. The slate and the glass are 3 to 4 m apart, so a reader at the glass and a reader at the slate must talk across the room or through a runner.

**The puzzle, exactly.**

The slate's marks are the lookout's code. The key is readable only with an eye at the spyglass: a small card is mounted inside the tube 10 cm from the eyepiece, lit by the window behind it, so that looking through the glass shows the card and nothing else. The card:

> ↑ town road ↓ Landing road
> | a bell of the church clock (count them)
> ✕ red coats ○ ours ◇ father
> ⌂ at the house

The standing orders painted by the window:

> ours on the Landing road: ONE
> boat ready: TWO
> father: nothing
> red coats on either road: BLACK, ONE, BLACK

The slate's last three rows (the rest of the slate is earlier days, same code, no decoys needed: it is a log):

> ◇ ✕ ↑ ||||||
> ✕ ✕ ✕ ↓ ||||||||||||
> ✕ ⌂ (and a smear where the chalk dragged)

The lantern's shutter slides between three detents: OPEN (the light shows), HALF (TWO is two short opens; the players do not need it), BLACK (the shutter covers the light). A magnet in the slider and three reed switches read its position.

Solution logic: the person at the glass reads the key aloud. The people at the slate read the last rows: red coats, many, on the Landing road, at twelve bells; then red coats at the house. The person at the glass reads the standing orders: red coats on either road means BLACK, ONE, BLACK. The lantern operator slides the shutter BLACK, then OPEN, then BLACK. The board accepts that sequence in that order within ten seconds of the first move; any other sequence does nothing and the slider is simply moved again (no destroyable state; 27 orderings, so guessing is slower than reading).

The first row (father, with a red coat, town road, six bells) is on the slate and is not needed to solve the room. Only the player at the glass has seen the symbols ◇ and ○. That row is the finale's SEEN piece.

**Four players at once.** At the glass (key and orders); at the slate (finds the last rows and counts strokes); at the lantern (works the slider on command); runner at the map (carries words between the glass and the slate, and confirms on the map that ↑ is the town road and ↓ the Landing road). The medium sends the Waitstill player to the glass by name: "Waitstill, the glass is yours."

**What physically happens on solve.** The lantern's LED goes dark on the final BLACK. From under the window, a speaker plays the fate beat, twelve seconds: a girl's voice, flat and steady like a lookout calling, "Red coats. Landing road. Twelve. They're at the gate. Deliverance, bar the door. I keep the post." Then wind. On the map, the house glows red from behind (one LED). The medium: "She stayed at the window. She showed the black. The last mark is red coats at the house." Then: "Bring her slate."

**The relic.** The slate, lifted off its nail. Its outline is painted at the Waitstill seat.

**The reveal.** Role: the lookout. Fate, partial: she was at her post when the soldiers reached the house.

**What it plants in its bearer.** The Waitstill player, and only whoever stood at the glass, has read the whole key: ◇ father, ○ ours, and the bells. In the finale, blind, she will have a sighted player describe the first row's marks to her ("a diamond, a cross, an up arrow, six strokes") and she will say what it means.

| Nudge 1:30 | "She's at the window, looking down the road to the water. The last thing she wrote is the last thing she saw." |

### 5.3 Room 3: Mindwell, the listener. The listening place.

**Time budget 3:15** (walk 0:15, read the room 0:20, key page and last entry 1:15, horn at the knot and fate beat 0:35, medium's line 0:10, walk back 0:15, place and return beat 0:25). Sim estimate for the gate: 75 s.

**Why it is in the story.** Mindwell listened through the wall to her father's study and wrote what she heard in the ring's cipher in a small book. The night of the fire she went to her listening place, under the stair, and kept writing.

**What the players see on entering.** A cramped space (a closet, pantry or under-stair cupboard) lit by one candle lantern on a shelf. The back wall is pine boarding with knots. Above it, a shelf rail with six wooden pegs. On the shelf, a small cloth-bound book, tea-stained, with a pencil on a string: the ear-book. On a hook, a listening horn: a tin funnel on a short tube with a wooden earpiece. A low stool. Scratched into the boards at child height, tally marks.

Figure (the back wall, front view): six pegs in a row along the top. Under the third peg from the left, 40 cm down, one knot ringed faintly by wear. Nineteen other knots scattered across the boards, none marked. The horn hangs at the right; the shelf with the book and lantern at the left.

**The puzzle, exactly.**

The ear-book has ten pages, each headed in plain English and written in the ring's cipher. Page 1 is the key, as a child's rhyme:

> Our vowels are ours and no one else's.
> D for A, W for E, M for I, R for O,
> and Mother keeps U.

(A→D, E→W, I→M, O→R, U→C; the substituted letters are written as capitals, which makes the cipher easy to see and decode in a first prototype. If rehearsal finds it too easy, write them in lower case.)

Pages 2 to 6 are earlier days, short and true, not needed ("Tillery at the fence again"). Pages 7 to 9 are headed "Hallows' Eve, forenoon, the study", with a second line each: "Tillery with father", "the officer with father", "father alone". Page 10 is headed "Hallows' Eve, night, under the stair" and reads:

> ThWy DrW bWDtMng Rn thW dRRr. DWlMvWrDncW hDs bDrrWd Mt. M Dm Dt my knRt, CndWr thW thMrd pWg. M hWDr fMrW.

Decoded: "They are beating on the door. Deliverance has barred it. I am at my knot, under the third peg. I hear fire."

Solution logic: decode page 10 with page 1; it names the spot. Put the horn's bell to the knot under the third peg and hold it there. A magnet behind that knot closes a reed switch in the horn's rim; after two seconds the board plays the fate beat through a small speaker behind the boards at that knot, quiet enough that only the ear at the horn hears it clearly. Any other knot does nothing. Guessing twenty knots at three seconds each is slower than decoding with two people.

Page 8, "the officer with father", is not decoded in this room; nothing asks for it. It is the finale's HEARD piece (section 7.3).

**Four players at once.** Key reader (holds page 1 open and calls the substitutions); decoder (reads page 10 word by word and says each plain word); horn (hunts the knot as the decode narrows, then holds it); lamp and pegs (holds the lantern close for the readers, counts the pegs, relays the words from the horn to the others, since only one ear fits the horn).

**What physically happens on solve.** Through the horn, fifteen seconds, muffled as if through a wall: fists on a door; a boy's voice, "the bar, help me with the bar"; a girl close to the horn, whispering, "Deliverance barred it. I'm at my knot. I'll stay small"; then crackle. The horn player relays it. The lantern on the shelf flickers once as the beat ends (the spirit's yes). The medium: "Under the stair. She stayed small, like she was taught. She heard the fire come." Then: "Bring her book."

**The relic.** The ear-book. Its outline is painted at the Mindwell seat.

**The reveal.** Role: the listener (the horn, the book). Fate, partial: she was under the stair when the fire came.

**What it plants in its bearer.** The Mindwell player has worked the cipher. In the finale, deaf, she will decode page 8 when the others hear which entry the fire is repeating and point to it.

**In-character hint lines.**

### 5.4 Room 4: Remember, the memorizer. The nursery.

**Time budget 2:45** (walk 0:15, read the room 0:20, rhyme, sampler and tiles 1:10, bar and the father's words 0:30, walk back 0:15, place 0:15; the fourth return beat is the turn, section 6). Sim estimate for the gate: 60 s. The lightest room on purpose: it is the last thing before the loudest beat (immersive principle 6).

**Why it is in the story.** Remember said the reports. The ring's reports had to be said to the boat-man at the Landing in a fixed form, as a challenge and answer, and she carried them in her head. She was eight.

**What the players see on entering.** A child's bed. On the wall above it, a cross-stitched sampler. On a small table, a hornbook (a paddle with a printed page under horn) and a wooden board the size of a tea tray with four long grooves of different lengths cut across it, a hinged bar along its top edge standing open, and a cloth bag beside it. The bag holds eight wooden tiles.

Figure (the recitation board, top view): a rectangle 40 × 30 cm. Four horizontal grooves, the top one longest and each lower one shorter. Along the top edge, a wooden bar on a hinge, shown lifted, with four short pins on its underside. Beside the board, eight tiles: four long ones lettered DELIVERANCE, WAITSTILL, MINDWELL, REMEMBER, each with a different jigsaw tab on its right end; four short ones lettered CARRIED, SAW, HEARD, SAID, each with a notch on its left end that fits only one tab. Each name tile has a small hole on top at a position that differs from the others.

**The puzzle, exactly.**

The sampler reads, stitched:

> Nothing is known till it is seen, heard, carried, and said.
> D. 1763 W. 1765 M. 1767 R. 1769
> C. A. wrought this 1775

The hornbook's page is the children's rhyme:

> D is for Deliverance, who carries the word.
> W is for Waitstill, who watches the road.
> M is for Mindwell, who hears what is said.
> R is for Remember, who says what is heard.
> Who goes? Answer in the order you were born, and the boat will come in.

Solution logic: pair each name with its verb (the rhyme says which; the jigsaw tab fits only the right pair). Lay the pairs in the grooves in birth order from the top (the sampler gives the years; each pair's total length fits only one groove, longest to shortest). Lower the bar. Its four pins drop into the four holes only when all four pairs are in the right grooves the right way round; one wrong tile and the bar sits proud. When the bar lies flat it presses a microswitch under the hinge.

There is no code. The order is the children's ages and the pairing is their jobs, both of which the players have been living in for fifteen minutes.

**Four players at once.** Rhyme reader; sampler reader (calls the birth order); two at the tiles (one pairs, one places). The medium hands the Remember player the bag.

**What physically happens on solve.** From under the bed, a speaker: a girl's voice, eight years old, reciting the answer flatly as drilled, "Deliverance carried. Waitstill saw. Mindwell heard. Remember said." A pause. Then, quieter and not drilled: "Mother? Father said to tell you. He said: whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four. He said, tell your mother." A breath. "I'm under the bed like you said. It's hot." Cut. Twenty-five seconds. The medium: "Under the bed." Then, to the Remember player: "Those words were put in your mouth. Keep them. You will be asked for them." Then: "Bring her board. Leave the bar down."

**The relic.** The recitation board with its tiles in and the bar down. Its outline is painted at the Remember seat; the board is also the frame the finale is assembled on (section 7.4).

**The reveal.** Role: the memorizer. Fate: under her bed when the fire came, the fourth of four inside the house, which is what the turn needs (5.6).

**What it plants in its bearer.** The Remember player has the form (four lines, name then verb, in birth order) under her hands, and the father's words in her ears, marked for her by the medium. In the finale, silenced, she assembles the report on this board and writes what cannot be read off a relic.

### 5.5 The return beat, same every time

Each return is the same ritual with different words, so that by the third it is familiar and the fourth can break it (immersive: make each return an advance).

1. The bearer sets the relic in its painted outline at that child's seat. Ten seconds.
2. The captain, who can see the table, lights that seat's candle (an LED candle in a glass) and plays the mother's line. Fifteen seconds. The candle stays lit: progress is persistent and visible.
3. The medium names the next door with the spirit's reason, and sends them out together. Ten seconds.

The mother's lines, recorded, one per return. She refuses what the players have seen; her belief narrows anyway, because each line she gives up is one more child not at the Landing.

## 6. The turn

Budget 2:15 from the fourth placement to the first free action. The immersive report's rule: the visual transformation may be simultaneous; everything the players must understand is not. The fourth placement arms the turn; the medium confirms and fires it once; a relic lifted and replaced cannot restart it (the captain's console treats the turn as a one-shot).

**Where everyone is before it fires.** The four players are in their seats, the Waitstill player with the wall behind her and the Deliverance player at her left. The medium stands at the north side. The four relics are in their outlines, four candles lit. The slate and chalk are in front of the Remember player. The printed objective card (18 pt, cream card, 15 × 10 cm, under the slate until needed):

> She means to put him in the fire.
> Give her the report, in the form: SEEN. HEARD. CARRIED. SAID.
> One voice says it whole.
> Each child she hears falls quiet.

**The curses, self-applied, with their safe forms.** Each is the object from the opening, already demonstrated; applying it takes a few seconds; removing it is always allowed and costs nothing (immersive failure table: a guest who declines or removes a curse gets the demonstrated alternative and no penalty).

The fiction loses nothing: the fire is already burning through the curses; what starts at "now" is her act, which she announces at that moment. The fire clock is 7:00 (call 3), which with the budget in section 9 keeps the worst case under thirty minutes and gives a group that plays in parallel about two and a half minutes of margin.

The arm is manual for this reason.

## 7. The finale

The finale is at the table, seated, in the fire's light and sound, for at most 7:00. The win is one spoken thing everyone watches, gated by four pieces worked in parallel and an assembly in the middle. Every piece has a cursed bearer whose contribution is necessary and a path through their restriction; the non-verbal channel is the slate and pointing, both used at the opening.

### 7.1 Her progress, observable on sound and light

Her act is a timer with three marks, each spoken by her and visible as a light step, so the muffled player sees it and the blind player hears it (progress and deadline readable independently, immersive principle 9).

| Fire left | She says | Light | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7:00 | (the objective has just been given) | FIRE-1: amber, slow movement | Bed at playing level; four child stems low; his voice absent |
| 5:00 | "Josiah." | FIRE-2: redder, faster movement; the centre candle (hers) turns from white to red | His voice enters the bed, far off, calling her name once a minute |
| 3:00 | "As you condemned me, I condemn you." | FIRE-3: red, the shadow wheel at full speed | His voice nearer, every thirty seconds |
| 1:00 | "The fire turns to him." (said by the medium if the mother's line is masked by the bed) | FIRE-4: pulsing | His voice calling continuously |
| 0:00 | Nothing | Black, three seconds | His scream joins the children's; a hard cut to silence |

The children's stems are the players' progress. Each child's scream stem drops out when that child's line is spoken in the testimony (7.5), and that child's seat candle turns from flame flicker to steady white. Taught in the objective card ("each child she hears falls quiet") and in the medium's sentence, so a silenced scream means heard, not lost (synthesis rule change 12).

### 7.2 The four pieces and who does what

The four relics are on the table. Each holds one line of the report, and each line can be got only through the child's bearer. The rule the players know from the sampler and from the four returns: what a child saw, heard, carried or said is given by that child's bearer; the mother takes nothing second-hand. In play that means four extractions, each a pair, run two at a time.

**Phase A (the first two pieces, in parallel, about 1:15).**

CARRIED, the sealed paper. The Deliverance player (gloved) and the Remember player (silent, with hands). He tells her: break the seal, find the arrow, open that flap first, then clockwise. She does it; the paper opens flat. He reads it aloud, since it is in plain English:

> By order. The children of Josiah Ashby, Esq., of Ashby House, Harrow's Landing, are not to be molested nor charged, their father having this day given his sworn evidence before the court. Capt. R. Fenwick, 31 October 1777.

His judgment: the fold, and reading it. Her hands: necessary, because his are gloved. Path through the restriction: voice to hands.

SEEN, the slate. The Waitstill player (blind) and the Mindwell player (deaf, sighted, can speak). The medium has put the slate in front of Mindwell. Mindwell reads the first row's marks aloud as shapes: "a diamond, a cross, an up arrow, six strokes." Waitstill, who alone read the whole key at the glass, says what it means: "Father, with a red coat, on the town road, at six bells." Mindwell reads the second row: "three crosses, a down arrow, twelve strokes." Waitstill: "Red coats, many, on the Landing road, at twelve." Mindwell cannot hear Waitstill's answers and does not need to; the Deliverance and Remember players can, and the Remember player writes. Waitstill's judgment: the code. Mindwell's eyes: necessary, because Waitstill's are covered and the other two are busy. Path: eyes to voice to the slate.

**Phase B (the second two pieces, in parallel, about 1:30).**

HEARD, the ear-book. The fire bed carries, every twenty seconds from the clock start, a muffled man's voice through a wall: the officer's sentence, "Swear to her treason, Ashby, and your children are not charged. Refuse, and they hang beside her." The Deliverance and Waitstill players, who can hear, catch words of it ("Ashby", "children", "swear"). They tell the Remember player, who opens the ear-book to the Hallows' Eve pages and points the Mindwell player to the heading that matches: "the officer with father". The Mindwell player decodes page 8 with page 1 and says it aloud:

> SwWDr tR hWr trWDsRn, Dshby, Dnd yRCr chMldrWn DrW nRt chDrgWd. RWfCsW Dnd thWy hDng bWsMdW hWr.

Her judgment: the decode, which she practised in her room. The hearers: necessary, to pick the page out of ten. Path: ears to pointing to eyes. Half of the group hears the fire; the one who must work the page cannot. That is the handoff's whisper box made playable.

SAID, from memory. The Remember player writes on the slate, from what she was told to keep in the nursery: "Father said: whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four. Tell your mother." Her judgment: recall and wording, under the fire, in chalk. Nobody else was told to keep it, though the others heard it; if she falters, a hearer may prompt her aloud and she still writes it (the rule is that Remember gives it; the rule is not that nobody may help).

### 7.3 The assembly: Remember's recurring work

The Remember player is the assembler. Through phases A and B she writes four lines on the slate, each as it arrives, in the form: name, verb, fact, and nothing else. The form is terse because chalk is slow; twelve words a line is the ceiling, and the medium says so in the nudge if a line runs long.

> Waitstill saw: Father, town road, six bells, with the officer. Red coats, Landing road, twelve.
> Mindwell heard: Swear to her treason and the children are not charged; refuse and they hang.
> Deliverance carried: The officer's protection for us four, bought with Father's oath.
> Remember said: Father said, whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four.

About fifty words; she writes while others work; she decides what each line keeps (judgment: she compresses the slate rows into a clause and the paper into a clause; the form only demands name, verb and fact). The slate goes face-up in the recitation board, which sits in front of her with its bar down: the board is the frame, the slate its page. When all four lines are on it she raises the board so the medium can see it. The medium reads it in silence and says one of two things: "It is whole. Say it." or "One is missing: [name]." That is the gate. The medium is the judge because she can see the slate and the players cannot all see it; the rule she applies is the sampler's, four lines, each with a name, each with a fact, and she applies it in the open.

The silenced player therefore works for the whole finale (writes three lines from others' speech and one from memory, chooses the words, orders them, presents the board) and is the one player who cannot be bypassed: nobody else may write her line, and in practice nobody else is free to write the rest.

### 7.4 The one last act everyone watches

The speaker is anyone with a voice who can see the slate: the Deliverance player (the natural choice, hands gone, voice free), the Mindwell player, or the Waitstill player if someone reads it to her line by line. The medium does not choose; the players do. The speaker stands if they like, holds the board or has it held, faces the centre candle, and says the four lines whole, in order. The other three are still; the medium is still.

As each line is finished, the captain drops that child's stem and turns that child's candle steady white: Waitstill quiet, Mindwell quiet, Deliverance quiet, Remember quiet. If the speaker stumbles and restarts a line, nothing is lost: the stem drops when the line is complete. The convergence is deliberate: four pieces in parallel, then one linear act (escape report 4.3 item 2).

### 7.5 The win confirmation

With the fourth line, the last child falls silent and only the fire and his voice remain. Then his voice stops too. The light steadies to FIRE-1 and warms. Her voice, close and clear: "Josiah." His, close for the first time: "Constance." Hers: "I did not know." His: "Nor I." The medium: "She heard him." The clock is cancelled on the fourth line; only one ending runs. Section 8 has the walk-out.

### 7.6 The failure set piece

At 0:00 the mark sequence completes: his scream joins the children's for two seconds, then a hard cut to silence and black for three seconds. In the dark the medium lights a hand lamp (a battery lantern at her hand, the first ordinary light since the turn) and speaks the tier. Nothing else happens to the players: no touch, no movement. The restrictions end at the lamp: "Take them off. All of you. It's over."

### 7.7 The tiered outcome

Credit is kept for what was done: every child whose line was spoken whole before the end stays quiet and its candle stays white through the dark (persistent, visible). The medium names each spirit's state. "At rest" is the handoff's six-spirit count made legible.

| Lines spoken before 0:00 | The medium says, by the hand lamp |
|---|---|
| 4, and the report finished in time | Win (7.5). "Six at rest." |
| 4 on the slate, the speaker cut off mid-report | "She had it in her hands and no one finished it. The four she heard are at rest. She is not. He is not." |
| 3 | "Three of her children were heard: [names]. [Name] was not. She would not take three for four. The fire took him. Three at rest, three not." |
| 2 | "Two were heard: [names]. Two not. The fire took him. Two at rest, four not." |
| 1 | "One was heard: [name]. The fire took him. One at rest, five not." |
| 0 | "None of them were heard. The fire took him, and she is where she was. Six unresolved." |

Then, in every losing case, the debrief in character and out of it (principle 29): the medium turns the slate to the group and says what the missing line would have said, so they leave knowing what they missed, and then opens the circle (section 8).

### 7.9 The real stop

Distinct from both endings: on "Cut" or "stop" from anyone, or the medium's judgment, the captain hits one button: entertainment audio off, light to full ordinary white, hazer off, and the medium says out of character, "We're stopped. Take everything off." It looks and sounds unlike the fire ending (which is dark and silent) and unlike the win (which is warm and candlelit). Resume only if everyone wants to; otherwise the medium thanks them and walks them out in ordinary light. The stop is tested at every rehearsal with the bed running and the muffs on.

## 8. The ending and exit

**The walk-out beat (1:30 after a win; 1:30 after a loss).**

Win: after "Nor I", her voice names the children, "Deliverance. Waitstill. Mindwell. Remember. Come," and the four white candles go out one by one as she says each name; then the centre candle; then the room's own light comes up slowly, warm (not the full white of the stop). The medium, in the ordinary light: "The circle is open. Six at rest. Take them off; thank you for your hands, your eyes, your ears, your voice." She lifts the slate from the board and gives it to the Remember player to carry out. The world has changed and there is a moment before the host speaks (principle 26).

Loss: the tier line by the hand lamp, the debrief, then the medium opens the circle in the same words minus the count, and the room's light comes up.

**What players walk out with (CAPTAIN CALL 18, default).** A sealed copy of the report in the ring's form, printed on the same cream card, folded in the ring's fold and sealed JA: "what the children never delivered." Fifty cents each; six made per night. The slate is wiped at the door by the medium in front of them, which is also the reset.

**The reset for the next group (target 8:00; 10:00 with one person).**

| Where | Steps |
|---|---|
| Hub | Wipe the slate; relics back to their rooms (four walks); candles reset from the console; lantern on; objects to seats; a fresh objective card under the slate; chairs straight |
| Kitchen | Section 5.1: brick closed, fresh sealed paper, route card refolded, satchel hung |
| Window | Section 5.2: shutter to OPEN, slate on the nail |

| Nursery | Section 5.4: bar up, tiles in the bag |

With a second pair of hands resetting rooms while the medium walks the group out and greets the next, the reset is about five minutes of wall time. Five groups at 25:00 plus 8:00 is about 2:45 of evening.

**What the first group may tell the fifth (CAPTAIN CALL 18, default).** The medium asks for one thing as they leave: "Keep her children's words to yourselves tonight." Everything else (that it is a séance, that you will be cursed, that there is a fire) is told by the house's reputation anyway and spoils nothing, because the first half's gates are physical and the finale's pieces are in the relics. The one thing that would spoil the finale is the four lines, and asking is the only enforcement available.

## 10. Safety

- **The blind player is seated before the fire and never moves.** Her seat has the wall behind it; the Deliverance player is her named guide, seated at her left with a hand on her forearm from the moment the veil comes down; the route from her seat to the hub door is walked with her at the opening while she can see. The mask comes off at will. Nothing is handed to her blind except the slate's edge if she asks for it.
- **Haze, not fog, and never at the cost of the alarms (call 17, default out for the first night).** If haze is in: a water-based hazer at low output, visibility over two metres at the table, nozzle more than fifty centimetres from anyone and never at the floor, a test run before the first group with the smoke alarms live. If an alarm trips in the test, no haze that night. Alarms are never disabled. The fire is light, shadow and sound.
- **No open flame.** Every candle is an LED. The hand lamp is a battery lantern.
- **The stop word is Cut; stop and ordinary distress language count too; Less lowers intensity without ending play.** Taught out of character at the door (section 3.1). The captain's stop button kills the entertainment audio and haze and raises full white light; the medium says "We're stopped" and ends every restriction. It is distinct from both the win (warm, candlelit) and the loss (dark, silent). Tested at every rehearsal with the bed running and the muffs on.
- **Hearing: rule versus real muffling (call 23).** Default: passive ear defenders of about 25 dB, which pass a shouted Cut and close speech and block the whispered loop in the bed. The muffled player's stop is visible (full white light) and the medium's check-in is a hand raised in her sightline, never a word. The alternative for a guest who declines is the rule form: nothing said aloud is for her.
- **The silenced player can always speak for safety**, and doing so is never called a broken rule.
- **Gloves come off instantly; no binding of any kind on anyone.**
- **Exits.** The hub's door to the hall and the front door stay unlocked and clear through the run. No spoke door is ever locked. The kitchen's "sealed" back door is a paper and a plank on a door that is not an exit, or a door flat; if the real back door is a required exit, it stays usable and the seal goes on a flat beside it.
- **Sound levels.** The bed never exceeds conversational speech at the table; peaks (the cry, the scream, the cut) are short and set in rehearsal with the four seats occupied.
- **The medium's job is scoped.** She never operates effects, never performs the mother, never leaves the group. The captain never enters the room during play. If the captain cannot see the table, a camera does it.
- **Consent.** Roles are assigned by consent at the door; a guest who wants none of the four restrictions takes the gloves, which are the lightest, or plays unrestricted with the rule form.
