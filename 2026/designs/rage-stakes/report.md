# Halloween 2026: why act at the fire?

Story proposals for the captain · revised after the 10 October voice-session findings · `hw-rage-stakes`

## 1. Recommendation

Choose **Let the children finish**. The mother means to make their father hear what his children suffered. She calls them back into the fire as his accusers. The guests have spent the evening carrying those children's unfinished work; now they must get their actual words heard before she makes their suffering the last word.

That gives the guests an answer to “Why should I stop her?”: **Because she is keeping the children in it. We came to speak for them.** They need not like their father, forgive him, or believe death can be undone. They can still say he deserves punishment. Their job is to finish the children's errand, and make their mother stop asking them to serve hers.

The revised backstory makes Deliverance the stopped courier carrying protection home, keeps his imprisoned mother away from the bargain, and gives the father a refused demand for a trial after the ashes. The finale brings those accounts together. It changes how she treats the children: they did their work, the adults failed them, and there is nothing left for them to prove.

Second choice: **The father has been speaking through the medium**. This puts a living person the guests know into immediate danger. It has the clearest urgent stakes, but adds a supernatural rule and asks more of Tamari's performance.

Two other directions are worth keeping: the mother can seize the faculties the guests lent the children; or the guests can deliver the report and deliberately let her burn him. That last option is a real alternative, not a wrong answer dressed as freedom.

These are four separate proposals. None is approved. The captain has chosen Rage over Resurrection; older documents that leave that choice open are superseded. This report recommends a story, not a new puzzle design.

## 2. A backstory that connects

The captain's later findings change the foundation of all four proposals. The old design put the mother beside her courier in town, then required her never to hear the explanation he carried. It gave an officer unexplained power, dispatched a destructive raid without an origin, and stopped the father's story at the ashes. Those are causal gaps. They cannot be repaired by a stronger final speech.

Here is one proposed sequence. It preserves the handoff's neighbor betrayal, coerced condemnation, failed protection, four deaths in or around the fire, and the father's forced participation in his wife's execution. The new connective facts remain proposals. This is fictional wartime coercion, not a claim about a historical officer's legal jurisdiction. [H §3; S1, S3, S4]

### What happened, in order

1. **The neighbor informs on her.** Call this person “the neighbor” unless a scene needs a name. The accusation reaches the Crown commander. He sends men to arrest the mother and a separate party to seize her papers, stop messages leaving the house and hold the household. The raid order precedes the husband's testimony. It is not secretly his order.

2. **The mother is already a prisoner when the bargain is offered.** She is taken away without the children. Deliverance is returning from an earlier courier errand, not standing beside her at an arrest in town. The commander sends the father a demand: confirm the accusation or the children will be charged with her. He must present it as an unbought oath: disclose the bargain and the protection is withdrawn. Mindwell overhears it at home. The father sees his children named in the demand and armed men enforcing it. His judicial office gives him no command over those men. The threat works through custody and force, not an unexplained claim that any officer outranks any judge.

3. **He goes to swear, believing he can save them.** Before leaving, he tells Remember, “Whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four. Tell your mother.” He cannot speak privately with his imprisoned wife. He gives his sworn deposition without her present. She is told what he has sworn; the bargain has been made outside her hearing. Keep those two events distinct. Nobody has to miss a conversation happening beside them.

4. **He obtains protection and sends it home.** Deliverance reaches the town after the mother has been confined. His father gives him the commander's written protection for the children, bought with the oath, and sends him to the house. The father is kept under guard to ensure he carries out the sentence against his wife. He cannot take the paper himself or visit her to explain. The children, if released, are the people he expects to carry his explanation to her.

5. **The order fails to arrive in time.** An outer picket stops Deliverance on the approach. The sentry holds him for the raid officer rather than accepting a countermand from a boy. Inside, the children have barred the door and hidden as their mother taught them. The raiding men set fire to the house to force entry and destroy the hiding places. That is their culpable escalation of the seizure order. Waitstill sees her brother held outside with the paper; she cannot reach him. When the fire spreads he tries to reach the children and dies at the house's edge. Three die inside; the courier dies trying to get in.

6. **Both parents remain ignorant of the failed protection through the execution.** The father has received no reply. He believes the written order has spared the children. He obeys the demand for silence at the execution because he still believes the children depend on it. She sees him condemn her and carry out his part, without hearing what bought his obedience. She dies hoping the children used the escape arrangements. Her ignorance comes from separation, his coerced silence and the stopped messenger, not proximity to an explanation she inexplicably misses.

7. **The father demands an accounting afterwards.** Released, he finds the four dead and the protection still undelivered. He files a sworn complaint against the command that gave the orders and demands a trial for those responsible. The authority that ordered the seizure refuses to act. He keeps pressing the accusation; he does not merely grieve, forgive the Crown or somehow possess the power to hang military officers himself. He puts the failed protection and the returned complaint in his son’s recovered satchel. His accusation goes unheard in life, just as the children's report never reaches their mother.

This gives him an action after the ashes and gives the present séance a purpose: someone may finally hear the witnesses. It does not acquit him of condemning his wife. His aftermath enters through his own refused complaint, folded with the protection paper, before the guests deliver their final report. The children do not magically know events after their deaths.

### Test the captain's stopped-courier sketch

Keep it as the recommended connective change, not as an adopted captain ruling. It makes the paper a failed delivery with a visible consequence. The weak point is the sentry: “he happened to be delayed” is not enough. Show the refusal and its cost in the lookout's account. The boy has the order; the men acting on the earlier order refuse to receive it. That is a specific wrong for the father's later complaint to name.

Drop the old conclusion “None of them left the house.” It is false in this version. The fourth return establishes “None of them survived.” Do not put the courier back inside merely to preserve an old recording.

The four returns still remove four hopes: he may have reached the boat; the others may have got around the blocked approach; hiding may have protected them; Remember may have escaped to tell the story. The last account closes that final hope. The mother turns on the husband whose condemnation she knows: the person she trusted with their children stood with the people who killed them. Soldiers are culpable, but his betrayal is personal. She has not heard his failed order or his later accusation. Her anger has an object before the guests know whether her judgment is fair.

The father's complaint is an added document in the existing relic, not a fifth collection or a new lock. Opening and interpreting the satchel now reveals both the bargain and what he did when it failed. The other three contributions establish the people and events his complaint concerns.

The assembled account can say: “Deliverance carried protection bought with Father’s oath; Father later kept it with his refused demand for a trial. Waitstill saw the boy held outside while the soldiers fired the house. Mindwell heard the threat: condemn Mother and conceal the bargain, or the children are charged with her. All four endings keep the self-applied curses and ten-minute clock. The sitting explains the borrowing convention; guests acquire its objects in the rooms and apply them at the turn. ## 3. Let the children finish

One sentence: she summons the children's suffering to condemn their father, and the guests must finish the report so she can hear her children instead of using them as accusers.

### The evening

At the sitting, the medium says, “A child who is still waiting to be heard needs someone to carry the words.” The mother's first request stays “Find them.” The guests' work gradually acquires a second meaning: someone has to bring home what each child could not.

**Kitchen:** the courier's account gives the failed delivery a recipient: “Show them Father's paper. Let us out.” The satchel carries the protection and the later complaint described in §2. **Window:** give Waitstill's existing recording a last instruction, “Tell her what I saw.” **Listening place:** Mindwell says, “I wrote it down so she would know.” **Nursery:** retain the father's words and finish Remember's appeal with “Will you tell her?” These replace lines; they do not add another exchange or task.

On each return, the mother briefly acknowledges the child's work before finding a new escape story: “He kept the message safe”; “She kept watch”; “She kept listening.” Guests can see the thing she values and the burden it puts on each child. The fourth breaks that rhythm. No one came home to report.

She turns toward him: “Josiah. You will hear them. All four. You will hear what they endured.” The fire brings back their unfinished calls. She wants witnesses against him, and she will not dismiss them until he has suffered too. Her love has become a demand that the children keep working for her.

The medium at the turn:

> “She is keeping the children in the fire to make him hear them. Finish their report. Make her hear what they came to say. You have ten minutes before she burns him and leaves their words unfinished.”

The guest stake, in one hearing: **“We promised to take their words home. If we do nothing, she keeps them crying in the fire.”**

### Win and loss

Keep the four contributions, with §2's changed content: protection and refused complaint; the stopped courier and fire; the threat; the father's words. These are connected witnesses, not a report the children somehow composed together. The guests finish assembling what never reached the mother. The father's later complaint proves that he too demanded an accounting; his silence was not his final response to the deaths.

Each spoken contribution receives a short acknowledgment from the mother: “I hear you, Deliverance.” That child's distress stops and the candle steadies. Acknowledgment releases that child from the duty to testify again. Written words alone do not earn this change.

The fourth contribution completes the account. She stops calling on the children: “You did all I asked. You need do no more. I will not call you back for him.” The fire recedes on that response. Her final address to him can be, “They would not hear you either.” This recognizes their shared demand for an accounting without cancelling what he did to her. There is no required pardon or reunited marriage. The children are heard and released; she abandons the punishment that kept summoning them. Her relationship to them visibly changes from commander to mother.

On loss, the father joins the remaining cries. Then the fire drops, but the unheard children's calls continue briefly. Revenge has not answered them. She calls one unheard child's name and receives the same unfinished request. The medium names the reports that arrived and the ones that did not, then closes the sitting. Voices already released stay quiet. The guests leave having accomplished something, but not their whole errand.

### Changes, strain and research

Beyond §2's common evidence changes, this needs the children's requests, an opening convention about hearing the dead, the return acknowledgments, and the turn and ending lines. The existing relics, report and final act carry them. No moving keepsakes to a safe border; no repeating the death discovery to break a loop.

The added fiction is that the mother's demand can keep unheard children answering. The handoff does not establish that law. Seed it at the sitting and demonstrate it in the finale. Do not claim the children knowingly died on a mission to vindicate their father; they died while doing the work already shown.

The strain is emotional: some guests will care little about fictional children. The job must still be satisfying as a completed delivery. More screams will not fix indifference. She must also seem capable of hearing new evidence, not magically defeated by a report she understands and rejects.

P8 is satisfied because her demand for justice for the children becomes a demand upon them. P9 is satisfied when she acknowledges their work, dismisses them and ends their distress. S5 supports attachment through performing their work; S24 warns against requiring grief; S9 supports the visible payoff. This fails S1 if release is introduced only at the last line. Finale patterns 3.1, 3.10 and 3.12 apply: knowledge acts, partial work matters, and the ending gets a descent.

## 4. The father has been speaking through the medium

One sentence: the father has been trying to answer through the medium all evening, and when the mother attacks him the living woman lending him her voice will burn with him.

### The evening

At the sitting, Tamari establishes her practice: “I lend the dead my voice. While I do, what reaches them can reach me. We finish the sitting together.” A man's brief, unnamed answer interrupts her before the first excursion. She repeats his words deliberately; she remains herself. He does not secretly possess her. Neither she nor the mother yet knows who he is.

**Kitchen:** use the satchel content from §2. **Window:** after the stopped-courier account, the man answers through Tamari, “He had their order.” **Listening place:** after the threat, he tries “I accused them—” and cannot finish. **Nursery:** retain “I did it for you four.” Tamari recognizes it as the man's earlier plea. These are short substitutions in the existing medium beats, not a second investigation.

Returns still take away escape routes. The mother increasingly hears this man's interruptions as excuses. The fourth return identifies him openly: “Josiah. You have been here listening.” His presence now has a purpose: he tried to bring the accusation while alive and is trying again through the medium. His refused complaint in the satchel supplies evidence for that claim. His wife has heard his interruptions as excuses.

“You lent your oath to their killers. Now you will feel their fire.” Tamari recoils as it reaches her voice. The immediate victim is the person who has guided and helped the guests throughout the evening.

The medium at the turn:

> “He is speaking through me. If she burns him, she burns me with him. Give her the children's report before she does it. Ten minutes. Their evidence is here.”

The guest stake: **“Save Tamari. The ghost she wants to burn is using her voice.”**

### Win and loss

The curses still fall on the four child bearers. Their cooperative report is the only available independent account. The father cannot settle this by repeating “I meant well.” As each child's contribution reaches her, the mother stops summoning that child and the candle steadies.

At the completed report she understands the failed bargain and his later effort to hold its makers accountable. Her decision concerns the living person in front of her: “No one else pays for what was done to us.” She releases her attack and tells the children they can stop answering. The fire ends; Tamari takes a clear breath and speaks in her ordinary medium's voice. The father can still be refused: “I have heard you. Go.” Release is not absolution.

At expiry his cry and Tamari's broken attempt to speak coincide; the fire cuts out. She lights the existing hand lamp but cannot produce words. The final supernatural cost is her voice, lost in the burning contact. Child contributions already heard remain quiet; unheard children still call once into the silence. Then Tamari explicitly leaves character to restore the group and end play. Her ability to host the exit does not retroactively heal the fictional medium.

### Changes, strain and research

Changes: an opening rule, three brief interruptions, recognition at the nursery return, and the attack and release performed through Tamari. The rooms retain their relics and use §2's shared evidence changes. The finale retains the same report and final utterance; its existing escalating father's voice now visibly affects the medium.

The handoff allows a medium, incomplete knowledge and recorded spirits. It does not establish that contact carries injury, or that the father has been present throughout. Both are additions. This version needs the first demonstration and plain turn sentence; suggestive acting alone is insufficient.

Tamari must remain able to guide the game while endangered. Her voice deteriorates at the ending, not throughout the solving period. The largest credibility question is why she cannot simply stop speaking: establish at entry that lending the voice lasts until the circle is closed. If that feels like a rule added to trap her, prefer Let the children finish.

P8 connects her vengeance to the same children she sought, then exposes its cost to another helper. P9 changes the mother from demanding another victim to refusing one, releases the heard children and visibly restores the medium. S8 supplies a present event happening to the guests' circle; S9 and S26 support the breath and voice as the payoff. S1 is strained by the added contact law. S10–S13 support responsive performance but do not prove this acting load is practical. Finale patterns 3.8 and 3.12 fit the personal consequence and release.

## 5. She keeps what the guests lent

One sentence: she uses the guests' borrowed hands, eyes, ears and voice to make the father experience his children's deaths, and intends to keep those faculties in the fire as permanent witnesses against him.

### The evening

At the sitting, the medium explains the loan: “You will lend what they cannot use. When their words are heard, the loan ends.” The objects are earned in the rooms; each acquisition lets one guest help where another cannot. The guests remain living helpers throughout; nobody becomes a dead child or a vessel for one.

**Kitchen:** Deliverance's message asks for hands to carry it. **Window:** Waitstill's existing call asks someone to bear witness to what she saw. **Listening place:** Mindwell wants someone to take what she heard to her mother. **Nursery:** Remember asks someone to speak the words she could not deliver. The child performances and objects make the loan useful before it becomes dangerous.

With every return, the mother addresses a guest by the child's name and thanks the faculty that brought the evidence. Preserve her hopeful explanations. The fourth return changes her purpose: the loans can no longer find living children, but she can use them against the man she blames.

She calls each name and the curses are self-applied: “Your hands will carry him. Your eyes will show him. Your ears will hear him. Your voice will call him guilty.” What seemed like temporary assistance has become a demand for service without an end.

The medium at the turn:

> “She means to keep the senses you lent, so his punishment never ends. Finish the children's report. Once she hears them, she must let their witnesses go. You have ten minutes.”

The guest stake: **“She'll keep our hands, sight, hearing and voice in that fire unless we finish the job we lent them for.”**

### Win and loss

Use §2's four-part account, including the refused complaint. The father has already tried to force a hearing; she is now forcing witnesses to remain instead. Each spoken account ends that child's need for a witness; its candle turns white. The objects stay on until the circle closes, preserving the shared final action. The mother can no longer treat the children's story as nothing but evidence for her sentence.

On the fourth line she addresses the living guests: “You have done what you came for. I will take nothing more.” She dismisses the children too: “Your words are home.” Their voices quiet, the fire ends and the medium calls each loan returned as the guests remove the objects. The mother gives up making others carry her vengeance.

At expiry the father cries out. For each uncompleted account, the mother's corresponding command sounds again in the now-dark house. The medium states which loans returned and which she kept. Removal of the equipment ends real play; in the story, some part of the guests remains serving as her witness. Do not make someone perform blindness or silence at the party afterwards.

### Changes, strain and research

The necessary changes are four requests in existing child beats, the opening loan convention, the turn's four commands, and endings that account for the loans. No new marks, collection objects or extraction puzzles are needed. The father is the ostensible target; the guests' own faculties are the instrument and the price.

This is the most direct use of the existing curses, and the largest addition to their meaning. The handoff permits binding but does not say borrowed faculties can be retained. Nor does it say that hearing testimony obliges the mother to return them. That limit must be established early, with the same clarity as the threat.

Its weakness is precisely that supernatural contract. The guests may focus on beating its terms and cease to care about the family. “She must release us” can also make her final change merely compulsory. The win therefore needs her voluntary decision to dismiss the children and stop the fire after the loans end.

P8 works if her demand is recognizably for witnesses to the children's wrong, not generic appetite for living souls. P9 requires both the returned loans and her release of the children. S1 and S12 support consistent supernatural limits; S24 warns that personal peril does not manufacture attachment. S9 is broken if the only payoff is taking off the equipment. Finale patterns 3.8, 3.10 and 3.11 fit, but the loan must never be presented as consent to a penalty the guests were not told existed.

## 6. Hear the children, then let her judge him

One sentence: the guests must get the children's report heard before the mother's sentence falls, then choose whether to ask her to spare the father or let his punishment proceed.

### The evening

Take her claim seriously. He condemned her. Coercion explains that act but does not erase it. She can be right that an account is owed and still leave the justice of burning him open. Nothing in the solution should force the players to call his actions good.

At the sitting, the medium establishes: “We bring the dead their words. We do not promise forgiveness.” **Kitchen:** disclose the protection and his later complaint. **Window:** use the stopped courier and the raid's violence. **Listening place:** retain the threat, including the demanded silence. **Nursery:** preserve his message and add Remember's request: “Tell her all of it.” These use §2's shared changes.

The returns keep stripping hope away. The mother first hopes he sent help, then discovers that neither his paper nor her instructions got a child out. At the turn: “He sentenced me. I will sentence him. But my children shall be heard first.” This is vengeance with a boundary she can honor: their words are not hers to replace.

The medium at the turn:

> “Get all four children's words to her before the ten minutes end. Then they can rest. She will burn him unless all four of you ask her to spare him. You can let her sentence stand.”

The guest stake: **“Get the children heard. Then we decide whether to stand between him and her fire.”** The children and guests do not secretly suffer more for choosing punishment.

### The actual choice

The report retains all four contributions and their visible acknowledgments. It reveals both the coercion and his later demand for a trial before any judgment. The guests can decide his attempted redress matters, or that it cannot answer his betrayal of his wife. Then Tamari asks each bearer for a response: intervene or let the sentence stand. Remember writes or points; the others can speak. This is the only added procedure, forced by giving the group a consequential choice.

If all four request intervention, the speaker asks her to spare him. Otherwise the sentence stands. This rule is declared at the turn, not invented when the group disagrees. Her own intended action is the default; the guests' unanimous petition changes it. They are not pretending to vote on behalf of the dead children.

Any unresolved response at the deadline leaves her sentence in place. That prevents an indefinite debate. Once all four have responded, the chosen ending plays immediately. Both branches use the completed testimony; neither awards a better score or a secret “true ending.”

### Win and loss

**Spare him:** the completed report releases the four children; the petition stops the father entering the fire. She says, “For those who carried their words, I stay my hand.” Then, to the children, “Come. You owe us nothing.” The father is left with what he did, without reconciliation promised.

**Let him burn:** the same completed report releases the children. Their lights steady and their voices fall quiet before his punishment. She says, “Their work is done. This is between us.” His voice enters the fire alone; hers remains with him. The guests have freed the children and declined to save him. The mother chooses vengeance over joining them in rest. That is a consequence, not a host's rebuke.

**Lose the errand:** time expires before all four accounts are spoken. His cry joins the unheard children. The mother acts without the whole report; some children remain unanswered. The medium names the completed deliveries and the unfinished ones. This loss differs from deliberate punishment in both sound and meaning: the guests failed to get the children out of the parents' dispute.

If the report is complete but the group cannot agree, the default punishment ending is an achieved errand with no collective intervention, not a puzzle loss. State that result honestly. Refusing to save him must not be coded as incompetence.

### Changes, strain and research

Changes: the opening promise, Remember's added request, the mother's boundary at the turn, four brief responses, and two authored success endings. Relics and curses remain; evidence follows §2. This adds performance branching and a judgment after the factual convergence; it is the largest change to the finale's shape.

The handoff wanted a clear mission rather than a major branching choice. This deliberately strains that preference in response to the captain's reopened question. It also asks guests to decide an adult moral question at a party. The petition rule is a proposed limit she accepts, not established history.

P8 preserves her pursuit of justice for her children. P9 is satisfied in both wins: she stops using their unfinished business to justify keeping them present; their voices are released. Sparing him changes her action, not necessarily her judgment. S9 supports paying off that initial job; S24 allows guests to feel differently about it; S28 warns against offering agency the performance cannot honor. Finale pattern 3.4 supplies the central cautions: explain the choice, make its costs real, and plan for disagreement. Do not use secret collateral harm to steer guests toward mercy.

## 7. Why the first direction wins

| Direction | Why guests act | What the mother must change | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Let the children finish | Complete the errand they have already taken on | Stop making the children serve her vengeance | Needs attachment and a seeded reason they remain unheard |
| Father through the medium | Save the living person helping them | Refuse another victim for the family | Adds dangerous contact and an acting burden |
| Keep the borrowed senses | Recover something of themselves | Return what was lent and dismiss her witnesses | Adds the most supernatural rules |
| Hear them, then judge | Free the children and choose whether to intervene | Separate the children's needs from her sentence | Adds a genuine moral branch and disagreement |

The first uses the relationship the evening has already earned. Guests have handled each child's work, carried it home and watched the mother evade its meaning. The finale completes those same actions under pressure. Their motive survives “I don't care about the father.” It also keeps the final revelation inside the winning act, which is the handoff's central demand. [H §12; S1, S5, S9]

Choose the medium version instead if the captain wants an immediate “save her” response with less dependence on care for absent children. Choose the judgment version if the desired memory is an argument about what the guests decided. Do not combine all four: a threatened medium, stolen senses, trapped children and a jury would obscure the single reason to act.

Directions discarded:

- **His ghost is the house's last support:** makes the father indispensable through a new metaphysical job unrelated to the family history.
- **Destroying him destroys the children's souls:** supplies instant stakes by inventing a second death and makes him a hostage to their survival.
- **Burn the house and everyone at the party:** clear danger, but the threat outruns the four-person scene and needs an explanation for why a ghost can burn the living house now.
- **Reveal that the father is one of the guests:** steals that guest's established child role and needs concealed acting or a second set of instructions.
- **Give him a second crime after the fire:** a cover-up or private execution could earn fresh rage, but needs another accusation and witnesses; prefer an aftermath that develops this failed bargain.
- **Complete a revolutionary dispatch to save unseen allies:** introduces another mission too late; the family's undelivered accounts already supply an errand.
- **Rescue the children from their historical deaths, resurrect them, or restart time:** contradicts the chosen direction or revives the rejected rescue/loop problems. The children can be heard now; their deaths remain true.
