# Rage: the guests' evening

You are four guests at a séance in Ashby House, each carrying the name of one of Constance Ashby's children. At the front door, Tamari tells you that the game becomes dark and loud and that each of you will later give up hands, sight, hearing, or speech. You can remove anything you wear or stop the game. At first you are here to find what became of the children. When their mother learns the answer, the medium gives you another task: “Before it reaches him, give her the report her children never gave: what was seen, what was heard, what was carried, what was said, and one of you say it to her whole.”

## Arrival

Tamari meets you before you enter. She is still herself. One of you will wear a blindfold while seated, with a friend's hand on your arm. One will wear earmuffs, one big gloves, and one will stop speaking. Nobody is tied. Nothing is locked.

She asks which restriction each of you is comfortable taking. “Cut” or “stop” brings the lights up and stops everything. “Less” lowers the intensity. You can take an object off whenever you want, and the game continues.

Then she becomes the medium and brings you into the living room.

## The sitting

A lantern burns at the centre of the table. Around it are four places marked with the outlines of a satchel, a slate, a small book, and a board. A plank carries four names: Deliverance, Waitstill, Mindwell, Remember.

The medium tells you the town's account of Ashby House. It burned on All Hallows' Eve in 1777 while the magistrate was away. People say his wife and children died in the fire. They say she set it herself, and that her ghost still calls for the children. This is the account the medium knows. The things you bring back will test it.

“She is bound to this table; she can't go where her children went. You can.” You are to go together, bring back what each child left, and put it where that child sat.

The guest carrying Deliverance takes riding gauntlets. Waitstill receives a black mourning veil. Mindwell takes black-covered hearing protectors. Remember has a ribbon at the wrist, chalk, and a writing slate.

When the spirit calls your child's name, you must use your object and keep helping with what remains. The medium calls Deliverance. The gauntlets go on. Picking up the chalk is difficult; Remember picks it up instead. The gloves come off. Remember writes the child's name and shows it around.

The spirit answers once for yes and twice for no. You hear the raps and see the lantern flicker. Remember writes the four names and points to each. Deliverance gets one rap.

“The courier,” the medium says. “The kitchen.” All four of you leave the table with her.

## Deliverance's room

In the kitchen, a paper seals the back door: nothing is to be removed, by order of the Crown. A satchel hangs beside it. A boy's cap rests on the hearth shelf. A kettle hangs from one iron; another iron sits low at the other side of the hearth.

Inside the satchel is folded paper. The Deliverance guest finds a small ink arrow and opens that flap first, then works around the fold. It is a note from his mother.

If soldiers come while she is away, he must bar the door and put what he carries in the hearth's hiding place. Then he must run for the Landing.

The note also tells you how to open the hiding place. One guest reads and calls the actions. Two pull the irons and hold them. The fourth pushes the brick behind the kettle.

The brick falls inward with a clack. Inside is another folded paper, sealed in red wax with the letters JA.

“He came home,” the medium says. “He put it in the drop, as she told him.” But the door is sealed from outside. “He didn't run.”

She asks you to leave the wax seal shut. The paper goes into the satchel, and you carry the satchel back to the living room together.

Deliverance puts it in the outline at his seat. His candle lights. Constance's voice comes from the room.

“He used the drop. Good boy. Josiah's seal?” She starts to ask what Josiah did, then stops. Deliverance ran, she insists. He went to the Landing as she told him.

Waitstill would have seen him go. The voice sends you to the window. You leave the satchel on the table and follow the medium into the dining room.

## Waitstill's room

A chair faces the street window. A spyglass points through the glass. A shuttered lantern stands on the sill. A slate hangs by the door, covered with marks. Between them is a child's map: the house, the town road, the Landing road, Tillery's field, the woods.

Waitstill looks into the spyglass. Inside it is the key to the slate's marks. Arrows name the roads. Crosses mean red coats. A diamond means Father. Strokes count the church bells.

Another guest reads the slate. A third handles the lantern. The fourth follows the roads on the map and carries words between the readers.

The last entries show soldiers on the Landing road at twelve bells, then soldiers at the house. The orders beside the window tell you what Waitstill must show: black, one light, black.

You slide the shutter closed, open, closed. The lantern goes dark. The house on the map glows red.

A girl's voice calls from beneath the window: “Red coats. Landing road. Twelve. They're at the gate. Deliverance, bar the door. I keep the post.” Then wind.

“She stayed at the window,” the medium says. She tells you to bring the slate.

Back at the table, Waitstill lays it in its outline. Another candle lights. Constance says the warning kept the boat away. So the children went by the woods. She repeats that they went by the woods.

Mindwell would have heard them leave. You go with the medium to the hall closet, leaving the marked slate beside the satchel.

## Mindwell's room

The closet is cramped. A candle lantern lights a cloth-bound book and a pencil on a string. Pine boards cover the back wall. Pegs run above them. A listening horn hangs on a hook; there is a low stool and tally marks scratched at a child's height.

Mindwell opens the book. Its first page gives a rhyme for replacing the vowels with letters from the family's names. Mother keeps U. The last page is headed Hallows' Eve, night, under the stair.

One of you calls the substitutions while another works through the words. Another takes the horn. The fourth brings the light close and counts the pegs.

The words tell you soldiers are beating on the door. Deliverance has barred it. Mindwell is at her knot, under the third peg. She hears fire.

You put the horn to that knot. Through it come fists on a door, a boy asking for help with the bar, and a close whisper: “Deliverance barred it. I'm at my knot. I'll stay small.” Then crackling. The guest at the horn tells the others what came through.

The lantern flickers once. “Under the stair,” the medium says. “She stayed small, like she was taught. She heard the fire come.”

You take the book back to the table. Mindwell places it in its outline; the third candle lights.

Constance calls her a good girl. Small under the stair, she says, the soldiers never found her. Then she stops short of what happened afterward. Remember has the words. Remember will tell you they went.

You leave the book with the other things and follow the medium to the nursery.

## Remember's room

There is a child's bed. A stitched sampler hangs above it. On a small table are a hornbook, a wooden board with four grooves, and a bag of tiles. A hinged bar stands open along the board.

The sampler gives the family's rule: “Nothing is known till it is seen, heard, carried, and said.” Beneath it are the children's initials and birth years.

One guest reads the hornbook's rhyme. Deliverance carries the word. Waitstill watches the road. Mindwell hears. Remember says what is heard. Another reads the birth order from the sampler. Remember takes the bag, and two of you pair and place the tiles.

Each name belongs with its work. The eldest goes first. When all four pairs are in their grooves, you lower the bar. It lies flat.

From under the bed comes Remember's voice, reciting: “Deliverance carried. Waitstill saw. Mindwell heard. Remember said.”

Then she speaks more quietly. Father told her to tell Mother: “Whatever you hear of me, I did it for you four.”

“I'm under the bed like you said. It's hot.” The voice cuts off.

“Under the bed,” the medium says. She tells the Remember guest to keep the father's words. You will be asked for them.

You carry the board back with its bar down. The four of you sit again. The satchel, the lookout's slate, and the book are waiting. Remember sets the board in the last outline.

## The turn: the fire

The fourth candle lights. The medium asks you to put your hands on the table and rings a bell.

Constance names the evidence: “The drop used. The black shown. Under the stair. Under the bed.” Then: “None of them left the house.”

Her cry follows. The candles flicker. Amber and red light move across the room. Fire crackles; four distant, wordless voices rise behind it.

The sound lowers for her next words. Josiah's seal is on the paper in her son's satchel. He swore against her; soldiers came, and the children burned. “Then let him burn as they did.”

Far off, a man calls, “Constance.”

The medium calls you in turn. Deliverance puts on the gauntlets. Waitstill lowers the veil, and Deliverance keeps a hand on the guest's arm. Mindwell puts on the hearing protectors; the medium points to the lantern. Remember tightens the wrist ribbon and takes the chalk without speaking.

The medium tells you to give Constance the report her children never delivered, whole, through one voice, before the fire reaches Josiah. Each child she hears will fall quiet. Mindwell can read the task on a card laid before them.

“Starting now.” You remain seated. Everything you brought back is in front of you.

## The finale

Deliverance tells Remember how to open the sealed paper. His gloved fingers cannot do it. Remember breaks the seal, finds the arrow, and opens the flaps as he directs.

The paper promises protection to Josiah Ashby's children because their father has given sworn evidence. Deliverance reads it aloud. His oath bought that promise.

At the same time, Mindwell describes the marks on Waitstill's slate. Waitstill cannot see them, but remembers the key from the spyglass. A diamond, a cross, an upward arrow, six strokes: Father with an officer, on the town road, at six bells. The soldiers come by the Landing road later.

Mindwell cannot hear the answer through the muffs. The other two can. Remember writes it.

A man's words repeat through the fire, as if through a wall: swear to her treason and the children will not be charged; refuse, and they hang beside her.

Deliverance and Waitstill listen. Remember finds the matching heading in Mindwell's book and points it out to the muffled guest. Mindwell decodes the officer's threat and says it aloud.

Remember writes the father's words from the nursery. He did it for the four children. Tell their mother.

Together, the four things say more than the seal did. Josiah left before the soldiers arrived. He swore because the officer threatened his children. The protection came home with Deliverance. Josiah meant his children to be spared.

Constance calls his name. The room grows redder. His voice comes nearer. “As you condemned me, I condemn you.” Later the medium says the fire is turning to him.

Remember keeps writing: each child's name, work, and part of the truth. The writing slate rests in the recitation board. When the report is whole, Remember lifts it for the medium to read.

“It is whole. Say it.” Deliverance takes the report to speak. The other three attend to the board, the voice, and the candles.

## The win

Deliverance reads each child's part aloud. With each complete line, that child's voice leaves the fire and the candle at that seat becomes steady white.

The last line ends. The last child falls quiet. Then Josiah's voice stops too. The light steadies and warms.

“Josiah,” Constance says. For the first time he answers close by: “Constance.”

“I did not know.”

“Nor I.”

“She heard him,” the medium says.

## The loss

In this ending, three complete lines reach her, but Remember's does not. Josiah's scream joins the remaining child's voice. Then everything cuts to silence and dark.

The medium lights a hand lamp. The three candles that turned white remain white. She names Deliverance, Waitstill, and Mindwell as heard. Remember was not. Constance would not take three for four. The fire took Josiah. Three spirits are at rest; three are not.

“Take them off. All of you. It's over.” She turns the report toward you and gives you the missing words: Father did it for the four children. Tell their mother. You leave knowing what did not reach her.

## The walk out

After a win, Constance calls the four names and says, “Come.” Each child's candle goes out as she calls it. Then the centre candle goes out and the room light rises.

“The circle is open. Six at rest.” The medium asks you to take off your objects and thanks you for your hands, eyes, ears, and voice. Remember carries the writing slate toward the door.

After a loss, the medium opens the circle after naming the spirits and showing you what was missing. The room light rises. All four of you leave together in either ending.

At the door, the medium wipes the slate in front of you. You receive a sealed copy of the report in the same fold you learned in the kitchen: what the children never delivered.

“Keep her children's words to yourselves tonight.” You walk out through the front door.

## Gaps

- The opening's fire-death account is false folklore. The handoff says the mother is executed and the husband is forced to participate in or carry out that execution. The design's private history instead says he witnesses her hanging. No guest scene tells you the execution story; this walkthrough keeps the folklore as folklore and supplies no missing scene.
- The design names Tillery as the neighbor who betrays her, but gives no required guest beat that explains his part. You see his field on the map; that does not tell you what he did.
- The design's losing-outcome wording conflicts over a report written whole but only partly spoken. The loss here uses its explicit three-lines-spoken ending, where the child's voice and candle agree with what reached the mother.
