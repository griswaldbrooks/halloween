# Resurrection: the guests' evening

You are four guests at a séance in the house built over the Hale family's cellar, each carrying the name and mark of one of Mercy Hale's children. At the front door, Tamari tells you to stay together and explains the gloves, scarf, muffs, and kerchief you will later put on yourselves. You can remove them or stop the game. At first you are here to find children their mother believes she sent to the river. When she learns where they died, she wants to put them into you. The medium gives you your task: “Send each child home by its own sign before its scream reaches you.”

## Arrival

Tamari meets you on the doorstep as herself. You stay together throughout the evening, and she stays with you. The front door remains the way out.

She asks who is comfortable wearing gloves, a scarf over the eyes, earmuffs, or a loose kerchief over the mouth that means no speaking. Each of you agrees to one. You will put it on and take it off yourself. You can take it off for any reason.

“Cut” or “stop” ends everything and brings up the lights. Nothing will touch you. There is no real fire. Then Tamari takes you to the dining room and becomes the medium.

## The sitting

Candles light the table. A black cloth covers something tied down at four corners. Beside the medium are a lantern, a framed writing slate with chalk, and a deck of cards.

The medium tells you what people say about Mercy Hale. She spied for the Revolution from the house of her Loyalist magistrate husband. A neighbor betrayed her. The tale says she hid her children before the soldiers came, refused to tell anyone where, and was hanged on All Souls' Day. The house burned. People say the children were never found.

The Goodales rebuilt over the old cellar and kept the children's things. The medium has asked Mercy where she hid them. “She will not tell me. I think she does not know.”

Four cards come face up. Deliverance, fourteen, carries messages. Waitstill keeps watch. Mindwell listens. Remember holds words in memory and says them back.

Deliverance receives a cord around the wrist with gloves hanging from it. Waitstill wears a scarf around the neck. Mindwell has muffs on a cord. Remember wears a loose black kerchief at the throat. Each of you tries the object on and off once.

When Mercy calls your child's name and says what she wants, you must do that thing and keep helping with everything else you have. Anyone who cannot speak can write or point on the slate.

The medium asks which knot should uncover the house. You ask the spirit. One rap answers. “The first. Deliverance.”

You hear Mercy for the first time: “Deliverance. Your hands.” Deliverance finds the tucked tail of the cord at that corner and pulls. The knot falls open. The cloth slides away.

Under it is a plan of the old house: door, hall, hearth, stair, chamber, cellar, and a river with a ferry. Its lights are dark.

“Find my children,” Mercy says. “I sent them to the river. Find where they went.” The front-door light glows amber.

The medium reads the light. Deliverance came home through that door. Her things are in the hall. All four of you get up and go with the medium.

## Deliverance's room

A satchel hangs from a ring on a coat post, held by a knotted strap. Across the hall is a sampler. A girl's coat hangs beside it. A lantern lights the room.

One guest takes the lantern to the sampler and reads aloud. The load holds. The tail frees. The tail is tucked away from idle eyes and pulled only when the courier is home.

Another finds a scrap in the coat pocket. It shows the knot, names its parts, and says to lift the load first or the knot binds. A third guest takes the satchel's weight. Deliverance finds the tail under its keeper and pulls.

The strap slips through the ring with a hiss. The satchel comes free into the other guest's arms. You can open its flap now.

Inside is a wooden token with a brass hand on its cap. A finder's tag says the Goodales found it in the ash inside the front door, under the bar.

Deliverance brought her mother's words home and barred the door. The thing you have just learned to undo is her courier's hitch. The sampler says releasing it means coming home.

You take the hand token back to the dining room. Deliverance puts it at the door on the plan. The amber light turns white.

“She came home,” Mercy says. “She had my words in that bag. She barred the door, the brave fool.” Then she says they went together, all four. Waitstill watched the road for them.

The window lights amber. “The parlour,” the medium says. You leave the token at the door and go together.

## Waitstill's room

The curtains are drawn. A lantern lights the sill. There is a black box with a lantern in its top, a child's slate on the chair, and a framed rule beside the window. Red, green, blue, and amber panes stand in a rack.

One guest reads the rule. Clear means quiet, green a friend, red that they are coming. Once and long means hold. Three short shows mean hide. From the house to the window, green once and long means the watch is over: come down.

Another brings the light to the faint writing on the slate. Waitstill records Deliverance running home, riders at Thatcher's, then riders at the gate. She shows red three times. Her last words are, “I wait for the house.”

You have the sign she gave and the sign she waited for. Nobody called her down.

Waitstill chooses the green pane and puts it in the lantern. The fourth guest holds the lever for a slow count and lets go. A green light shows once, long. The latch clicks; you lift the lid.

Inside is the eye token. Its tag says it was found at the upper window, east.

You carry it back together. Waitstill places it at the window on the plan. The light turns white.

Mercy repeats what her child saw and showed. “She waited for me to call her down... I was at the courthouse. Nobody called her down.”

But she still says Waitstill took the others and left. Mindwell would have been at the wall, listening. Mindwell would have knocked.

The hearth lights amber. You go with the medium to the kitchen wall.

## Mindwell's room

A small door is shut in a wooden panel. Beside it is a brass listening funnel, with a tube ending in two earpieces. A framed card gives the knock signals. A wooden knocker waits beside the door.

Mindwell and another guest listen together. One guest reads the card. The fourth waits at the knocker.

Through the tube come kitchen sounds, wind, and a child's knocks. Three, a pause, one. The two listeners count them again and agree.

Three and one means “they are here.” The house can answer one knock for stay, or two and two for “heard; come out.”

The guest at the door knocks two, pauses, knocks two. The latch clicks and the little door swings open. The ear token lies inside. The tag places it in the chimney cupboard.

This is where Mindwell listened and warned the others. The answer you have just given is the answer she did not get that night.

You return to the table with the token. Mindwell puts it at the hearth; its light turns white.

“She knocked that they were here, and no one knocked back,” Mercy says. Then she finds another way the children might have left. Mindwell would have taken the little one down. The cellar door opens toward the river. Mercy showed them the way over and over.

“Remember is eight. Find Remember.” The cellar lights amber. The medium brings the writing slate, and you follow her to the cellar door. Nobody goes down the stair.

## Remember's room

A panel on the closed door shows a house with little doors of its own. Pictures mark a barred front door, a hall with coat pegs, a hearth, stairs, a bed, and a cellar with barrels. A lantern hangs nearby. The card beside it gives the memorizer's walk.

Hear the message once. Place one piece in each room as you walk through the house. Then say it back by walking. After that, the paper goes into the fire.

One guest reads the route. Another brings the lantern close and names the pictures. Remember opens the little doors. The fourth writes down what appears behind each one.

Door, hall, hearth, stair, chamber. The pieces read: “Hear it”; “once”; “hold it”; “whole”; “say it.” The cellar door clicks open. Inside is the end: “to none but her.”

The medium asks Remember to say the drill back. “Hear it once. Hold it whole. Say it to none but her.” You have watched a message kept in the order of the house.

The mouth token is in the cellar. Its tag says it was found by the bulkhead door, which was barred.

You bring it and the writing slate back to the dining room. Before you place the token, the medium asks you to look at the plan. Three white lights mark the door, window, and hearth. The cellar is amber. The river is dark.

Remember places the token. The cellar turns white.

“Where were they?” the medium asks. You give the four places: the door, the window, the wall, the cellar. “And the river?”

Nobody.

## The turn: the fire

All four of you sit. The medium waits until your hands are free and the slate is on the table. Then Mercy speaks over silence.

“The door. The window. The wall. The cellar. None of them reached the river.” She says they were all in the house when it burned.

Fire begins low. Ember-colored light flickers. Shadows move on the walls. Haze drifts through the room. The four white places on the plan pulse.

“Then I will have them back,” Mercy says. “One into each of you who carries their mark. Eldest first.”

The medium asks everyone to hold still. Mercy calls each child. Deliverance's hands are hers: gloves on. Waitstill's eyes are hers: scarf up. Mindwell's ears are hers: muffs on. Remember's voice is hers: kerchief up, loose over the mouth.

Waitstill stays seated, with hands at the table edge and Deliverance as guide. The same objects you tried at the sitting now carry the curses.

The medium explains that Mercy taught her children to answer the network's signs before any voice, even her own. You learned each sign in its room. Give a child its sign and it goes home. Mercy cannot hold what she taught to let go.

Each child's scream is coming closer through the fire. If it reaches the guest with that mark, she has that child. The bearer knows the sign; another guest must give it. A child going home leaves the fire, and a light comes up at the river. Then you carry its token there.

The medium shows Mindwell the slate: “SEND THEM HOME, D W M R.” She points to the pulsing places and the dark river.

“Deliverance first. The cord. Who has hands?”

## The finale

A cord holds the hand token at the door. Its hitch is like the one on the satchel, with the tail tucked and a weight pulling it tight.

Deliverance can tell you how to release it, but the gloves cannot untuck the tail. The guest lifts the weight while Remember frees the tail and pulls. The knot spills and the token lifts out.

Deliverance's voice leaves the fire. A soft bell sounds. The door light goes dark; a white light appears at the river. “Deliverance is home,” the medium says. You move her token there.

While those two work, Waitstill listens for something else in the fire: Remember's voice, saying pieces of a message and naming rooms. The blind guest repeats the pieces for the others. When Remember's hands are free, Remember writes them on the slate. Mindwell can read what is shown there; the words from the fire do not count for the guest wearing the muffs.

Now Waitstill tells the sign from the window: “Green. Once. Long.” Mindwell picks the green pane; Deliverance holds the lantern's lever. The long green show ends. Another child's voice leaves the fire, another bell sounds, and another river light appears. You move the eye token.

Remember keeps gathering the message. In the hall: “to the river.” In the cellar: “for no one.” At the door: “go.” In the chamber: “wait.” At the hearth: “tonight.” On the stair: “all of you.” The pieces come out of order.

Mindwell can still speak. The guest tells Deliverance to listen at the hearth on the board: three and one, then answer two and two.

Deliverance bends toward it. The knock comes. The gloved hand answers. Mindwell's voice leaves the fire; the hearth goes dark and the third river light shines. A sighted guest carries the ear token to the river.

Waitstill keeps checking the message by ear. Remember puts its pieces in the walk order learned at the cellar door: door, hall, hearth, stair, chamber, cellar. Remember cannot speak the finished line, but can point to it on the slate.

The fire now carries one child's voice. Deliverance reads what Remember has ordered: “Go to the river tonight, all of you. Wait for no one.” Everyone attends to that line and the board.

## The win

“She hears you,” the medium says.

Remember's voice fades from the fire. The fourth river light comes up. The mouth token joins the other three there. The fire falls to embers; the four places in the house are dark.

Mercy repeats her message: “Go to the river tonight, all of you. Wait for no one.” Then: “They heard me. They heard me, and they stayed.”

After a pause: “Let them go, then. Let me go.”

“She's let go,” the medium says. Four tokens stand at the river.

## The loss

In this ending, Deliverance's sign comes too late. Her scream reaches the guest carrying her mark. It stops, and a child's voice answers, “Here, Mother.” The door light turns red.

“She has Deliverance. Keep going. The others can still go home.” Deliverance keeps helping. You send Waitstill, Mindwell, and Remember home by their signs, but the red place remains.

Mercy calls the roll. Deliverance answers from the board; the guest with that mark sits still, hands on the table. At each of the other names there is silence and a river light.

“Then I keep what I have.” The fire cuts away.

The medium closes the sitting. She puts her hand flat on the table before Deliverance and says the guest's real name. The red light goes out. She names the result: three children went home; Mercy kept Deliverance. The guest is released. The child remains in the fire.

She tells you the sign you missed: lift the weight, untuck the tail, pull it. You know both what you saved and what you left behind.

## The walk out

In either ending, there is one more paper. The medium takes a folded letter from the card box. The Goodales kept it with the children's things. Josiah wrote it to Mercy and never sent it.

“I have said what they asked of me. They promise me the children for it. God forgive me, I believe them. Go to the river when the children are safe. J. H.”

Now you hear why he confirmed her as a spy. He believed the promise would save the children. Mercy says his name once: “Josiah.”

The medium tells you the circle is closed and asks you to take off what you were given. Gloves, scarf, muffs, and kerchief come off before anyone stands. The room lights rise warm. After a win she names what you did: sent all four home before their screams reached you. After a loss she has already named the children she kept.

Each of you takes the card with your child's name. At the front door, the medium writes that child's sign on its back.

What she asks you to pass to later guests is simple: stay together, ask her when you are stuck, and the thing you were given is the thing you will need. You walk out together.

## Gaps

- The folklore says Mercy's husband sat in judgment on her. The handoff specifies that authorities pressure him to confirm her treason, then force him to participate in or carry out her execution. The letter gives his motive, but no guest beat gives the coercion at the execution or explains that the soldiers had already been sent. Those facts are not added to his letter.
- The failure scene says guests were taught at the opening to sit still for the final roll if taken. That instruction is absent from the written opening. The loss above includes the prescribed stillness; the sources do not supply the earlier line that teaches it.
