# Immersive performance, horror, larp, and cooperative game design

Research scout, 2026-10-08. Four guests, one live medium, a hoped-for twenty-minute run. This report follows the October 7 handoff and the captain's October 8 clarification: séance hub, one child’s spoke and return at a time, four returns, then the fire and curses. All applications below are proposals, not approved changes. No puzzles are designed here.

## Findings

The strongest model is a small cooperative game with theatrical transitions. Promenade theatre supplies ways to focus attention and invite participation; it does not automatically supply a mission, progression, or a win condition. The useful combination is: a clear initial task, repeated actions that teach the group how this world responds, discoveries that change its understanding, a visible turn, then a new task that uses what the group has learned.

Three findings matter most:

- The four returns can make the story intelligible. Each return needs to change something players can understand and use. Four equally weighted lore speeches would produce repetition, not development.
- Curses need to redistribute useful work. Taking an ability away without giving its owner a necessary contribution makes that player a burden or spectator.
- A fixed soundtrack cannot independently react to solves. A deadline track and separately controlled progress sounds can. The audience also needs a way to distinguish progress from time remaining.

The timing needs reconciliation. A ten-minute finale is half of a twenty-minute experience, not its last quarter. If investigation occupies three quarters and the finale lasts ten minutes, the whole run is roughly forty minutes before any additional time outside that split. The old simulation's 25–28-minute estimates concern superseded rules, but its warning about unbudgeted movement and performance still applies. This is arithmetic, not a measured estimate of the new game.

## Sources and what they teach

These are actual production pages, practitioner accounts, rulebooks, and technical or safety guidance read during this scout. Sources describe different kinds of experience; their practices are not interchangeable. Marketing claims are identified as such. Source identifiers below link to the pages so the recommendations can be checked.

### S1. Punchdrunk: Sleep No More

[Production page](https://www.punchdrunk.com/work/sleep-no-more-new-york/). Author: Punchdrunk, the producing company; directors Felix Barrett and Maxine Doyle. Primary evidence for the production's format, not a design manual.

- Guests choose their routes through the Macbeth-based world.
- The production treats the audience's journey as part of the experience.
- Movement and discovery provide agency without a published collective victory condition.
- Its listed two-hour format is a poor direct pacing template for this house game.

### S2. Third Rail Projects: Then She Fell

[Production archive](https://www.thirdrailprojects.com/thenshefell). Author: Third Rail Projects; created by Zach Morris, Tom Pearson, and Jennine Willett. Primary account of a production that ran thousands of performances.

- Only fifteen audience members attended each performance.
- One-to-one encounters, keys, objects, and discoveries gave guests concrete invitations to participate.
- The environment carried information, not just decoration.
- Its extensive performer and production credits matter: intimate audience handling does not demonstrate that one person can operate an equivalent show alone.

### S3. HER Cooper: Iterating Interactions

[Practitioner account](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/iterating-interactions/). Cooper co-created Strange Bird Immersive's work and performs Madame Daphne. The article reports revisions to interactions with paying audiences.

- Requests to participate need testing just like puzzles.
- Asking strangers to invent or disclose a secret initially failed with most groups.
- Easier questions and a rehearsed conversational structure improved participation.
- Changing a teasing challenge into a personal request changed audience behavior.
- Actors' reports drove revisions; the writer's intention was insufficient evidence that a scene worked.

### S4. DARKFIELD: SÉANCE

[Production description](https://www.darkfield.org/seance). Author: DARKFIELD, creators David Rosenberg and Glen Neath's company; the page credits Tom Lyall's performance. Primary production description.

- A twenty-minute sonic performance can establish a séance within a tightly bounded setting.
- Suggestion and audience belief are central materials.
- A recorded performance can carry supernatural presence without a visible ensemble of ghosts.
- This is evidence for sensory theatre, not for a puzzle-solving objective or player-controlled ending.

### S5. DARKFIELD: access information

[General access](https://www.darkfield.org/access), [show-specific warnings](https://www.darkfield.org/access-triggers). Author: the operator responsible for admitting and briefing audiences.

- A compulsory safety briefing exists outside the dramatic premise.
- Advance information describes seating, mobility, darkness, and sound.
- SÉANCE uses darkness without flashing lights; darkness and strobing are separate choices.
- The company explicitly acknowledges limitations for hearing-impaired audiences.
- Touch tours and written briefing arrangements demonstrate that orientation can precede surprise.

### S6. Geffen Playhouse: Play Dead

[Production archive](https://www.geffenplayhouse.org/shows/play-dead/). Author: presenting theatre; written by Todd Robbins and Teller, featuring Robbins and directed by Teller. Primary production record.

- A principal host can unite storytelling, illusion, and audience interaction.
- Sight, sound, and touch belong to the same staged experience.
- The listing disclosed haze, strobe effects, disturbing material, and interactivity.
- The seventy-five-minute production and full credits do not establish a solo-operator or twenty-minute model. Borrow its hosting function, not an assumed staffing plan.

### S7. HER Cooper: Bottlenecks: Designing for Focus Mid-Experience

[Article](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/bottlenecks/). Primary design account from Strange Bird's séance escape-room work.

- Put essential revelations where every player can attend.
- A completed collection makes a natural shared stopping point.
- Reveal the next activity after the crucial scene, not before it.
- Keep shared scenes brief; involve everyone when a single task controls progress.
- Lighting can help focus attention; sound alone did not reliably stop their players talking and searching.

### S8. HER Cooper: 100 Séances Later

[Article](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/100-seances-later/). Reflection after one hundred performances of The Man From Beyond: Houdini Séance Escape Room.

- The actor must respond to actual audience behavior, including withdrawal.
- A small physical change, a handle, eliminated a recurring hint.
- Hints preserve enjoyment when frustration overtakes discovery.
- Atmospheric detail can accidentally look like a clue.
- Rehearsal and repeated observation give a performer capacity to notice the audience.

### S9. HER Cooper: Bookends: Fulfilling Finales

[Article](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/bookends-fulfilling-finales/). Primary account of designing both winning and losing scenes.

- Show how the players changed the fictional world.
- Let them experience the consequence before returning to ordinary conversation.
- A losing team also receives an in-world ending.
- Resolution has both a factual job and an emotional job.
- A long epilogue is the author's production choice, not a requirement to copy.

### S10. Number One Escape Room / Third Gate Escape: horror design

[Designer’s guide](https://numberoneescaperoom.com/how-to-build-a-horror-escape-room-a-designers-complete-guide/), August 24, 2026. Corporate byline, TEC / The Escape Creators; written in the owning designer's voice. Practitioner evidence, with commercial claims not independently audited.

- Alternate fear with quieter intervals for thinking.
- Its operator reports that excessive fear causes guests to miss text and stop communicating.
- Leave enough quiet after a scare for a complete spoken sentence.
- Test timing with real groups rather than accepting a conventional booking length.
- Live actors can adjust to a group; a predetermined effect cannot make that judgment.

### S11. Leonard Pickel: Common Haunting Mistakes and How to Fix Them

[Article](https://www.hauntrepreneurs.com/haunting-articles/design-articles/common-haunting-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them/), with [Getting Started seminar outline](https://hauntrepreneurs.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/New-Getting-Started-Seminar-pt-1-and-2.pdf). Pickel is a haunt designer, consultant, and HAuNTcon founder/organizer.

- Design the audience action and scare before dressing the room.
- Give actors an effective position and mechanism instead of expecting performance to rescue poor layout.
- Sightlines and circulation determine what guests experience.
- An actor blocking a route damages throughput.
- Consult building and fire authorities early. His commercial-haunt guidance is not a determination of requirements for this private house.

### S12. Haunted Attraction Association: C.H.A.O.S.

[HAA events and safety program](https://hauntedattractionassociation.com/haa-events). Author: industry association running the Certified Haunted Attraction Operator Safety program.

- Safety is treated as trained operational work.
- Certification requires individual attendance, not borrowed staff credentials.
- Its Top Haunts program requires current trained staff at each location.
- The public page is a training overview, not a fog, blackout, or blindfold engineering standard. I found no public HAA/HAuNTcon text that licenses combining those effects here.

### S13. Texas State Fire Marshal: haunted-house guidance

[Builders and operators guidance](https://tdi.texas.gov/fire/haunted-house-builders-operators.html). Author: state fire authority. Concrete operational reference; its jurisdiction and cited code edition must not be represented as local approval.

- Exits remain unlocked and usable without special knowledge.
- Emergency illumination replaces confusing show conditions.
- Emergency announcements take priority over entertainment sound.
- Staff rehearse emergency responsibilities.
- Its recommended stop controls disable sound, visuals, and fog while bringing up lights.
- Its fog advice addresses false alarms; that is not a respiratory-safety finding.

### S14. Actors’ Equity Association: theatrical smoke and haze

[Regulations and linked studies](https://actorsequity.org/resources/producers/safe-and-sanitary/smoke-and-haze). Author: performers' union, publishing exposure guidance backed by commissioned studies.

- Evaluated products are specific combinations of machine, fluid, and attachments.
- Exposure depends on release duration, distance, and operating conditions.
- The equipment-based method has limits on how its tested cue is used.
- Other arrangements require production-specific monitoring within Equity's framework.
- The claim “theatrical fog is safe” is too broad. These employment rules are a technical reference, not a certification of this house or its audience.

### S15. Larp Design: Creating Role-Play Experiences

[Full revised PDF](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/136330/978-952-03-2260-1.pdf). Knudepunkt 2019 book, revised PDF 2021; edited by Johanna Koljonen, Jaakko Stenros, Anne Serup Grove, Aina D. Skjønsfjell, and Elin Nilsen. Read relevant chapters, not the entire book. Practitioner collection hosted by Tampere University.

- Koljonen, pp. 235–237: rehearse opt-out and calibration tools; unobtrusive signals can fail under stress.
- Danny Wilson, pp. 238–244: select mechanics for the intended experience; avoid stacking incompatible rules.
- Wilson distinguishes telegraphing, steering, and control of fictional behavior.
- Anni Tolvanen and Irrette Melakoski, pp. 316–320: design for competing speech and room acoustics, not just playback.
- Their Inside Hamlet example required changes to musical structure, speaker placement, and relative levels before transition cues became reliable.

### S16. Eleanor Saitta and Bjarke Pedersen: The Interaction Engine

[Essay](https://www.nordiclarp.org/2024/06/25/the-interaction-engine/), 2024 Knutpunkt writing. Authors are professional participatory designers; Pedersen describes his own PAN design.

- Start with what players actually do, expressed as verbs.
- PAN's possession necklace tells its wearer and witnesses how to behave.
- A physical token can coordinate self-applied fictional effects without continual adjudication.
- Predetermined story endpoints can coexist with unscripted interaction.
- That form depends on an agreed play style. A predetermined tragedy is not evidence that secretly unwinnable competitive objectives are fair.

### S17. Maury Brown: Creating a Culture of Trust

[Safety and calibration account](https://www.nordiclarp.org/2016/09/09/creating-culture-trust-safety-calibration-larp-mechanics/), 2016. Brown co-created New World Magischola; describes techniques developed with Sarah Lynne Bowman and Harrison Greene and revised across its runs.

- A spell recipient's consent matters even when the character is subject to magic.
- “Cut” stops play; “Largo” lowers intensity.
- They replaced “brake” because English-speaking participants could confuse it with taking a break.
- Speech and gesture alternatives are needed when one channel is unavailable.
- Opting out carries no fictional or social punishment.

### S18. Lizzie Stark and freeform GMs: How to Debrief a Freeform Game

[Interview article](https://lizziestark.com/2012/07/15/how-debrief-freeform-game/comment-page-1/). Stark is an author and participation designer; contributors include American designer Emily Care Boss and experienced Nordic freeform GMs.

- Leave a buffer between intense fiction and ordinary social life.
- Let players describe their experience before the GM gives an authoritative interpretation.
- An exit ritual can separate player from character.
- Feedback can reveal failures invisible to the GM.
- Contributors differ about format and timing; debrief is not a demand for public emotional disclosure.

### S19. Steel Crate Games: Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes

[Official game description](https://keeptalkinggame.com/), [Bomb Defusal Manual](https://www.bombmanual.com/web/index.html), introductory rules pp. 2–4 and sample modules. Authors: the game’s developers.

- The defuser sees the object; experts have instructions but cannot see it.
- Roles need each other to interpret information and act.
- Modules have explicit completion feedback.
- The deadline and strike rules specify defeat.
- The reference material carries complex conditional rules so players need not memorize all of them beforehand.

### S20. Ben Kane / Steel Crate Games: balancing the two roles

[Verified developer AMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/pcgaming/comments/ebhrd3/we_are_steel_crate_games_creators_of_keep_talking/), December 2019; answer to “Did you have to actively balance the challenge level...”. First-person design evidence, not an anonymous player theory.

- Workload between roles was tuned through public playtesting.
- Long solitary reading or manipulation damages the intended back-and-forth.
- Authored mission pools control difficulty while allowing variation.
- Distractions respond to progress and are withheld from struggling teams.
- Short rounds provide relief from exacting communication. This is the clearest source found on how much work to give each side.

### S21. Kasper Lapp / Sit Down!: Magic Maze

[Publisher rulebook](https://sitdown-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/MM_Rules_EN_HR_Sept2018_LD.pdf), especially pp. 2, 4–7. Primary rules.

- Players share a board but own different permitted actions.
- Silence restricts communication, not useful participation.
- A shared attention pawn supplies a deliberately narrow communication channel.
- Timer flips permit discussion, with the clock still running; acting ends the discussion.
- Introductory scenarios teach rules progressively, including an optional talking first game.
- Gathering all four items changes the goal to escape and disables a former ability: a clear mechanical turn.

### S22. Antoine Bauza / R&R Games: Hanabi

[Publisher rules](https://www.rnrgames.com/Content/RRGames/images/ProductRules/hanabiRules.PDF). Designer Bauza; English rules editors Frank DiLorenzo and Anthony Rubbo. Both image-based PDF pages were inspected.

- Players see others' cards but not their own.
- A clue supplies one constrained category of information and must identify every matching card.
- At four players, each holds four cards; complexity is bounded and visible to teammates.
- Giving information, discarding, and playing compete for a turn.
- Expiring the deck produces a scored ending, while three mistakes produce defeat. Imperfection need not erase every accomplishment.

### S23. Henry Smith / Sleeping Beast Games: Spaceteam

[Official site](https://spaceteam.ca/), [developer FAQ](https://spaceteam.ca/faq/), and [developer's App Store description](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/spaceteam/id570510529). Primary explanations of the digital game, not the later card adaptation.

- Instructions are time-sensitive and players have differently labelled controls.
- Communicating the command is part of the challenge.
- The FAQ identifies audiovisual confirmation when a control is set successfully.
- Some emergencies demand a synchronized team response.
- Its promised tone is shouting and chaos. Copying that communication load will affect a séance's mood.

### S24. Catherine Stippell / Pandasaurus: Nyctophobia

[Accessible rule transcription](https://www.64ouncegames.com/pages/nyctophobia). Original designer and publisher are credited; host 64 Ounce Games produces board-game accessibility materials. Primary rules in an accessible reproduction.

- Blackout glasses apply to seated players manipulating a tactile board.
- The sighted hunter guides a player's hand to their piece and clarifies what they touch.
- Shared cardinal directions give reports a stable vocabulary.
- The hunter is also a facilitator responsible for a fair, enjoyable game.
- Finding the car is followed by a call for help and survival interval; goal completion is observable.
- The instructions explicitly permit the hunter to moderate competitive play for the group’s experience.

### S25. Bomb Squad Academy: distinguish the examples

[Systemic Games' digital game](https://www.bombsquad.academy/) and [Escape Games Bonaire's physical offering](https://www.escapegamesbonaire.com/). Authors: their respective developers/operators. These share a name; they are not evidence of a common rule set.

- Systemic describes increasingly difficult circuits under time pressure.
- Cutting or switching incorrectly can trigger failure: actions have comprehensible consequences.
- Bonaire frames its physical game as a final examination with a suspicious bag and a defusal deadline.
- The physical listing gives a mission without a long lore preamble.
- Neither retrieved description establishes hidden-information roles in that physical game. Use KTANE's rules and developer account for that claim, not this name.

### S26. Figure 53: QLab show-control documentation

[Group cues](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/fundamentals/group-cues/), [fading audio](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/audio/fading-audio/). Author: software maker. Technical evidence for a cue architecture, not a recommendation to buy or deploy this product.

- A timeline group starts its child cues from one trigger.
- Pre-waits position events relative to that start.
- Fade cues adjust selected running audio rather than restarting a soundtrack.
- Different sounds can therefore have separate progress controls while sharing a start time.
- Cue organization does not itself detect solved puzzles or guarantee safe fog operation.

## Principles a builder can use

These are design rules synthesized from the sources, not universal laws or decisions for this game.

1. **Specify the audience's job.** Exploration is a valid theatrical activity, but a cooperative game also needs an actionable shared goal. State what to do and what counts as finished. [S1](https://www.punchdrunk.com/work/sleep-no-more-new-york/), [S19](https://www.bombmanual.com/web/index.html).
2. **Teach participation before raising its cost.** Rehearse the smallest version of an unfamiliar action, then alter its conditions. Do not ask novices to learn a new communication system during the loudest reveal. [S3](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/iterating-interactions/), [S21](https://sitdown-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/MM_Rules_EN_HR_Sept2018_LD.pdf).
3. **Give revelations an audience.** Converge active work before essential information. Do not reveal the next attractive task until the group has received the current scene. [S7](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/bottlenecks/).
4. **Balance exchanges, not word counts.** A role's information should prompt useful interaction soon. A whole page for one player and a button for another is not inherently balanced. Time the back-and-forth with players. [S20](https://www.reddit.com/r/pcgaming/comments/ebhrd3/we_are_steel_crate_games_creators_of_keep_talking/).
5. **Keep an available path through each restriction.** Hidden information, silence, and lost sight work when the remaining actions are legible. Audit who can receive, interpret, transmit, and act on each necessary fact. [S22](https://www.rnrgames.com/Content/RRGames/images/ProductRules/hanabiRules.PDF), [S24](https://www.64ouncegames.com/pages/nyctophobia).
6. **Separate fear peaks from difficult interpretation.** Reduce competing effects when players need to understand an instruction. Build intensity back once they know what to do. [S10](https://numberoneescaperoom.com/how-to-build-a-horror-escape-room-a-designers-complete-guide/).
7. **Make the fiction enforceable by the players.** Define a trigger and a specific response. “Be possessed” needs translation into permitted behavior. [S16](https://www.nordiclarp.org/2024/06/25/the-interaction-engine/).
8. **Give the performer controllable work.** Automate repeatable coordination; reserve live judgment for attention, hints, readiness, and distress. This is an adaptation of actor responsiveness and grouped cueing, not proof that solo operation is safe. [S8](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/100-seances-later/), [S26](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/fundamentals/group-cues/).
9. **Make progress and deadline independently readable.** A solved state should persist. Escalation should not reverse that signal accidentally. Distinct module status and timer displays in KTANE supply the precedent. [S19](https://www.bombmanual.com/web/index.html).
10. **Design defeat as an ending.** The team can lose its fictional objective while receiving a complete performance and a humane return to the party. [S9](https://strangebirdimmersive.com/immersology/bookends-fulfilling-finales/), [S18](https://lizziestark.com/2012/07/15/how-debrief-freeform-game/comment-page-1/).
11. **Safety overrides the fiction in every role.** A silence or hearing rule cannot remove the ability to call or receive a stop. Test the stop with the actual effects running. [S17](https://www.nordiclarp.org/2016/09/09/creating-culture-trust-safety-calibration-larp-mechanics/), [S13](https://tdi.texas.gov/fire/haunted-house-builders-operators.html).

## What this means for our game

The recommendations in this section are our applications of the evidence. Proposed speech is example wording, not a source quotation. Prop mechanisms, clue contents, and final puzzle logic remain for a later design pass.

### Séance opening: establish a contract and a first job

The incomplete folklore and an ignorant medium are useful. Tamari can learn with the guests, and her changed reaction can authenticate discoveries. However, ignorance of the history must not become ignorance of how to participate. The actor knows how play works even if her character does not know why the mother is here.

One opening option is to establish: “She is asking us to find what became of her children. Bring each keepsake back here, so we can ask her together.” This states the collection, return point, and purpose without explaining the twist. Give each guest a small, concrete participation task during the ritual rather than demanding improvised backstory or a long personal confession. This follows the accessible invitations described by Third Rail and Cooper [S2–S3].

Teach curse handling before the séance becomes threatening. Each guest can identify their object and demonstrate how they would activate and remove it. The future meaning can remain concealed. Knowing how to use a prop does not require knowing why the ghost will command it. An out-of-character safety introduction followed by an in-character ritual is compatible with professional sensory theatre [S5].

The rule “If the spirit calls you, obey it” is too broad as an operating instruction. Proposed replacement structure: when your child's name is called with the agreed curse instruction, apply your demonstrated restriction; continue helping through your remaining channels; remove it immediately for discomfort or a real stop. Preserve the supernatural tone in performance, but teach that bounded rule plainly beforehand. Token-mediated behavior has a direct larp precedent [S16].

### Four spokes: make each return an advance

The captain's together-out-and-back structure provides shared evidence and a recurring place where Tamari can check comprehension. It also limits what a lone performer must monitor. Retain it as the settled structure.

For each spoke, the later design document should fill the same fields: the group's current question; what action it can attempt; the evidence and keepsake returned; what the mother now believes; and what becomes available next. This is a progression specification, not four puzzle proposals.

The first return should make the return ritual familiar. Later returns should change its significance: accumulating evidence should narrow possible explanations and alter the mother's response. The fourth must complete a conclusion players recognize, rather than have a recording announce an unrelated surprise. There is no obligation to introduce a new rule on every trip. Repeated mechanics can carry changing meaning. This applies S3, S7, and S16.

The risk is spectator time. Four people travelling together do not guarantee four people playing. During a rehearsal, record who contributes during each spoke and who merely watches. Do not compensate for inactivity by adding longer speeches. Reserve necessary group scenes for the table, and keep attention on the discovery until its consequence is understood [S7].

### The fourth relic and the fire: one trigger, several ordered beats

The collection completing at the table is a strong theatrical trigger [S7]. The handoff's proposed simultaneous fire, screams, lights, fog, four curse instructions, and new objective is a poor instruction-delivery moment. The visual transformation may be simultaneous; everything the players must understand should not be.

Proposed cue sequence:

| Beat | What players perceive | What Tamari controls |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth return is complete | All four keepsakes are present; the latest evidence establishes the children's fate | Check that everyone has returned and is safely positioned; arm the turn |
| Mother understands | A concise response to what the group actually found | Start the coordinated light and sound transformation once |
| New threat becomes clear | The mother's intention is stated or demonstrated | Lower competing sound for the actionable instruction |
| Curses take effect | Each player recognizes and applies a familiar restriction | Confirm readiness and use the demonstrated substitute if needed |
| Final task starts | The objective, deadline, and first available action are clear | Start the playable countdown, or use a declared introduction interval within it |

If the fiction demands that the clock begins with the fire, include the instruction and activation interval in that ten minutes and rehearse its cost. Otherwise, begin the timed challenge after readiness. These are timing options; the report does not choose one.

Make the fourth-placement trigger mean “ready to transition,” not “fire effects no matter where everyone is.” A manual confirmation lets Tamari accommodate a dropped item, an equipment problem, or a guest who is still standing. Repeated placement must not restart the turn. This is proposed control behavior derived from the staging requirement; it has not been implemented or tested.

### How the medium could hand over the final objective

All options need the same four pieces: the danger, the action, the deadline, and visible confirmation. Tamari should remain capable of repeating the instruction in plain language. The character's fear may color delivery; it must not conceal the task.

- **Medium recognizes a ritual she already knows.** Establish her knowledge of opening and closing séances earlier. At the turn she recognizes that the mother is using the connection differently. She can specify the counteraction without suddenly knowing the historical truth. Best fit for preventing possession or closing a loop.
- **Mother reveals her intention; medium translates its practical consequence.** The mother's recorded statement supplies the new threat. Tamari directs the guests to the already established apparatus and explains the task. Best fit for an explicit antagonist, provided the translation is justified by observable evidence.
- **The recovered evidence changes what the ritual requires.** Players bring the decisive fact; Tamari restates the inference and identifies the performable response. Best fit for testimony or resolving the father's betrayal. The evidence must be present before the victory action, not explained afterwards.

A useful script template is: “She is trying to [intent]. Before [established deadline cue], you must [winning action] using [already introduced objects]. You will know it worked when [confirmation]. Start with [available action].” Brackets intentionally remain unfilled: writing a convincing speech cannot settle the underlying mechanics. These options apply S3, S9, and S19.

### Curses: retain competence, change the route

These are alternatives for later prototyping. The safety substitutions are not easier punishments or failed roleplay. Choose role assignments with the guests before play; do not randomly impose a restriction they cannot use.

| Child | Candidate restriction and useful role | Safe boundary / lower-impact option | Failure to catch in rehearsal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deliverance / hands | Loose bulky gloves inhibit fine work; the player can still interpret, direct, and make deliberate coarse actions | No binding. Gloves come off immediately when needed. Alternative: a rule against manipulating designated small game pieces | Everyone bypasses the player because their only function was operating an awkward object |
| Waitstill / eyes | A readily removable sleep mask at a seated work area; tactile information and spoken guidance remain available | No blind navigation, stairs, reaching into unknown hazards, or mandatory surprise touch. Alternative: screen only the relevant visual information while retaining environmental vision | Essential input or feedback exists only in print; a teammate performs the whole role instead |
| Mindwell / ears | Rule-based inability to use the spirit's auditory clues; other players relay meaning visibly, while Mindwell retains interpretive or operational responsibility | Prefer a game rule over actual sound blocking for an initial prototype. Optional removable muffling needs a proven emergency channel and a visible curse/start cue | The player misses their activation, objective, progress, or stop because all are audio-only |
| Remember / voice | No ordinary game speech, but gestures, pointing, writing, and handling objects remain available | No gag. Safety speech always works. Alternative: restrict only specified fictional speech, with ordinary clarification permitted | Required exact testimony can only be spoken by Remember, making victory illegal under the rules |

These implementations extrapolate from the limited channels in Hanabi, Magic Maze, and Nyctophobia [S21–S24]. They are not those games' safety instructions for a room-scale experience.

Do not fix the silent role by quietly expecting players to break the rule at the ending. If another person can voice Remember's contribution, or a known event releases the restriction, establish that possibility before the climax. Likewise, a deafened player's essential contribution should not be hearing a better whisper.

Each role needs an answer to “What can I do that matters now?” Private information is one possibility; a permitted action or interpretation is another. A role should require recurring judgment, not merely custody of a card another player can read once. Conversely, do not force secrecy when the fiction offers no reason for it. Hanabi and KTANE announce their information rules; they do not expect players to guess them [S19, S22].

For information sizing, start a prototype with one current responsibility per role and a short, accessible reference. That is a proposed starting constraint, not a researched numerical optimum. Keep the current objective, progress, and communication rules shared. Count how often each player receives something, decides something, and sends something useful. If one person is silently processing while everyone waits, reduce or redistribute that work. If everyone must shout across everyone else, serialize part of the exchange. Steel Crate's account supports this empirical approach [S20].

### A ten-minute audio finale

A prerecorded track can reliably supply a deadline, but increasing loudness is not a clock. Players cannot know that a particular fire sound means “nearly over” unless its meaning is taught or explicitly stated.

Proposed architecture, based on grouped cues and independent audio fades [S26]:

- A master timeline carries the fire's progression, intelligible time warnings, and an expiry event.
- Separately controlled stems represent unresolved tasks or spirits. A confirmed solve changes the relevant stem without seeking, stopping, or restarting the master timeline.
- A short acknowledgment plus a persistent prop or light state confirms the solve. It remains legible to someone who misses the sound.
- A victory cue cancels the scheduled defeat and presents success immediately. A timeout enters the losing ending. Only one ending may run.
- A separate real stop silences entertainment, stops fog if present, raises usable lighting, and ends the restrictions.

Silencing a child may mean rescue, death, or possession to an unbriefed listener. Establish the meaning and add affirmative feedback. Keep enough audible and visible urgency to show that the shared deadline remains active. This is a design inference from separating status and timer in KTANE [S19].

A single flattened audio file cannot remove an individual voice at arbitrary solve times. It can only play predetermined changes. If the available setup cannot mix stems, retain the fixed bed and let Tamari supply event-driven confirmation instead. No new show-control platform is required by this report.

The tested question is whether people can understand one another in the actual room. The book's sound chapter makes clear why louder cues alone are insufficient [S15]. Rehearse with four people speaking, costumes, the final speaker positions, and the proposed curse equipment. The ten-minute limit is a design variable, not evidence that this workload fits.

### One live medium: scope the job honestly

Tamari can plausibly keep a single group on a shared route, acknowledge returns, offer hints, and trigger prepared scenes. A practical script should mark what starts a scene, what player response it needs, a short recovery line for confusion, and how it moves on. Write the hint in terms of the object or evidence her character can perceive, so it helps without making her omniscient. Cooper's performance notes support responding to the actual audience rather than forcing a scripted response [S8].

Do not assign her simultaneous duties that require different locations or attention: secretly touching one guest, monitoring a masked guest elsewhere, operating fog, and performing the mother. Recorded spirits reduce the visible cast; they do not remove monitoring work. A solo-performer rehearsal must include quiet groups, frightened groups, a curse opt-out, and a missed cue. Reduce the effects or arrange separate operational help if she cannot observe and respond. None of the cited professional productions establishes that a lone host can safely cover every technical duty.

The hub helps only if Tamari can see the whole active play area or pause the group while moving with it. The room layout has not been established in this scout, so supervision coverage remains unproven.

### Safety with darkness, sound, fog, and touch

The handoff correctly forbids making a blindfolded guest navigate a hazardous room. Extend that principle: physical exit and real-world communication remain possible throughout play. For this prototype, keep the curse action at an uncluttered table and keep circulation legible. If touch is used, agree its permitted form beforehand and offer a no-touch equivalent. A production warning that touch happens is not, by itself, permission for any particular contact [S5–S6, S17].

Use a rehearsed plain stop such as “Cut,” accepting “stop” and ordinary distress language too. “Largo,” or a plainly taught “less intense,” can reduce intensity without ending play. Do not copy a visual-only check-in for the masked player. The voice-restricted player can always speak for safety; the hearing-restricted player needs visible notice and, only if agreed, a tactile notification. All restrictions end before an exit or evacuation. These are proposed adaptations of Brown's multiple-channel approach [S17].

For the fire illusion, light, shadow, and sound can work without fog. Fog remains an optional effect pending equipment-specific and site-specific assessment. Do not disable smoke alarms to preserve the show. Equity's guidance does not support an arbitrary “safe fog duration,” and HAA's public training page supplies no such limit [S12–S14]. Tamari's exposure also repeats across groups.

The real stop must look and sound unlike the fictional fire ending: normal usable light, plain instructions, entertainment stopped. Exit access must not depend on winning. The Texas guidance is evidence for this operating pattern, not a statement of which commercial attraction regulations govern the captain's home [S13].

### The three finale questions: options to develop, not decisions

The handoff's sequencing is sound: determine intention, failure, and the winning act before writing finale puzzles. These options compare the existing directions. They stop at the kind of terminal action and its consequence; they do not specify solutions.

| Direction | Mother’s active intention | Possible visible fictional defeat | Kind of winning action to define next | Main design burden |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resurrection / possession | Put her children into the four living guests | At expiry, her address to the guests changes from strangers to children; the ritual's state indicates possession, then the show releases the real guests | A collective physical action that interrupts or severs the established transfer | Explain why she can compel curses but can still be resisted; establish what exactly constitutes an interrupted ritual |
| Rage / vengeance | Carry out a specified destructive act against the father or his representation | The act completes; the audience hears or sees its irreversible result | Players physically present or deliver the decisive testimony through an established channel, causing her attack to stop | The target and imminent act are still undefined. A final letter alone would move the resolution back into passive exposition |
| Trapped fire / historical loop | Force this encounter to repeat the fatal event | The room returns to the opening state with the loss unresolved | Complete or change the previously established unfinished action that releases this occurrence of the loop | Define what survives between repetitions and how players recognize that this run has ended despite the fiction repeating |
| Rescue | Hold or reclaim the children within the recreated fire | Their manifestations remain caught as the fire reaches its endpoint | Move the established representations to an established safe condition, with a visible response | State that this frees spirits or memories, unless the fiction truly permits changing historical deaths |

These are applications of a changed-world ending [S9] and an explicit goal/deadline contract [S19]. No option is established by external precedent as the correct ending for this family story.

The father's motive can be integrated if knowing it changes the final permitted action, target, or interpretation before victory. If the group can win identically without encountering it, the motive is epilogue material. That may be dramatically worthwhile, but it does not meet the handoff's requirement for the final revelation to matter mechanically.

### What happens when players fail

Different forms solve this differently. Promenade theatre can complete its performance even if a guest misses material; a participatory tragedy can announce a fixed ending while leaving interaction open; timed cooperative games define loss explicitly; a séance escape room can stage a dedicated losing scene [S1, S9, S16, S19, S21–S22]. Our game should distinguish those contracts.

Proposed handling by failure type:

| Failure | Response that keeps the evening intact |
|---|---|
| A spoke stalls | Tamari gives increasingly concrete, fiction-consistent help. If a bypass is necessary, acknowledge assistance and carry the essential evidence forward. Do not let a missed early action delete the whole finale |
| Players misunderstand a rule | Clarify it directly. Confusion about permitted actions should not masquerade as a clever challenge |
| A guest declines or removes a curse | Use the demonstrated alternative; no fictional death or penalty for self-care |
| A prop or cue fails | Use a rehearsed manual acknowledgment or substitute delivery of the same information. Equipment failure is not player defeat |
| The finale clock expires | Run the losing scene for the selected premise, make the outcome unmistakable, then restore ordinary conditions |
| A real stop is needed | End fiction immediately. Resume only if the people involved want to and conditions permit; otherwise conclude outside character |

A losing ending can be short and still complete: consequence, clear end of haunting, ordinary light, removal of apparatus, thanks and an opportunity to talk. Do not require players to pretend they are still possessed at the party. Preserve credit for what they accomplished without relabelling defeat as victory. These proposals extend Cooper's losing scene and the freeform exit/debrief practices [S9, S18].

A forced tragedy is also a possible artistic choice, but it would need a different promise: participation in a doomed encounter, rather than a fair timed chance to prevent it. That would be a captain-level change from the handoff's explicit mission-and-outcome goal, not an invisible pacing adjustment.

## Next design work supported by this research

The immediate dependency remains the existing three-sentence finale decision. This report supplies options and constraints; it does not ask for a new decision before the research can finish.

After that choice, the most informative rehearsal is a bare-table run of the full sequence with four uninformed players and Tamari. Measure opening, each out-and-back, transition, finale, and release separately. Record missed instructions, idle players, accidental rule violations, assistance, and outcome. Test the actual restricted channels before adding full horror intensity. Then introduce effects and compare communication and timing. A successful state-machine run alone cannot establish comprehension or fun.

Before a detailed build proposal is credible, it should be possible to show: everyone can state the objective; everyone has an available contribution; every indispensable message reaches an impaired recipient; each solve has unambiguous feedback; the host can run both endings; the real stop works; and the entire sequence fits the chosen time budget. This is a proposed review test derived from the sources, not a claim that the present design has passed.
