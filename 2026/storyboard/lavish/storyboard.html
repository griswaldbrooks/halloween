01
PARLOR · ARRIVAL · 2 MIN

Four invitations. Four names.

Everyone begins together. No one has been chosen to survive.

Theater / low-build4 players · 4 offering tokens · 0 seals
“Her inheritance has waited for four living witnesses. Tonight, it can be yours.”

The medium is genuinely trying to help and does not know the ghost’s purpose. The medium tells the true tragedy plainly: a secret patriot, her Tory husband, four children murdered, a burned New England home. The colonial mother is the ghost, not the fortune teller. A later spiritualist circle promises access to her inheritance.

What players do

Give the map: four seals → return to the table → open the inheritance. Deal four transferable roles and one small name token per guest. Ask each player to speak the name written on their token; use that chosen name in the finale. Mark all four tokens on a four-place family register. Put four spendable offering tokens in the group purse. Required seals/keepsakes are a separate inventory.

Reset estimate: Roles, name tokens, map and purse: 1–2 min.

Operator cue & idea provenance

Captain is the medium, Tamari the attendant. Show the closed chest. Optional baquet chain + glass tone replaces 30 seconds of exposition. A secret Tory-spy Menace overlay goes to one randomly dealt guest, independent of public roles: one wrong suggestion or brief delay, never physical obstruction. A separately dealt Healer overlay holds one proposed cure token; four guests cover all roles, with no extra player. Keep the two special overlays on different guests.

Captain: four guests, card reading, colonial mother and later medium. Firstmate: inheritance/war-chest lure and keepsakes. Captain now confirms mediums are unwitting and menace serves the Tory husband. New draft: chosen name tokens, four-place register and shared offering purse.

02
PARLOR · VEILED CURSE + HONEST ESP TEST · 3 MIN

The house names its price

The first contact costs sight. The first test offers a worse bargain.

Theater / low-build3–4 players after this cost · survival undecided
“The card has chosen. Now tell me what lies behind the next five.”

The opening reading uses four honestly shuffled tutorial cards, exactly one Veiled. Whoever draws it is blinded immediately, teaching everyone how curses work. The recipient is not selected by the actors and gains no survival protection. Explain blindfold participation before the random draw; if a guest cannot participate, adapt the role openly rather than secretly fixing the draw. The cursed guest remains blind if they survive.

What players do

Then guess five real symbol cards consecutively, using five equally likely symbols. Return and reshuffle the card before each next draw, including after a correct guess. With independent uniform draws, five correct is 1 in 3,125. The cards and outcomes are honest; the demand is effectively impossible, not literally unwinnable. A lucky perfect run succeeds and pays no cost. On failure choose one cost: an offering token; an eligible curse; or one player. The collectors physically remove a sacrificed player and they leave the game. Everyone else advances. Subsequent genuine puzzle success avoids a cost; failed challenges use this same menu.

Reset estimate: Blindfold exchange/cleaning, curse ledger and rig: 1–2 min.

Operator cue & idea provenance

The blindness persists if that guest survives. If they are removed, give their personal ending aloud while still blind, declare their game over, then help them remove the blindfold and return to the party. Others can later receive Veiled too, subject to solvability. Never require the blind guest to be the one sacrificed.

Captain: at least one blind player, sacrifices during play, no guaranteed survivor count. “Feels closer” direction: costs can be curses, offerings or people. Captain decisions: randomly assigned immediate tutorial blindness, honest near-impossible card test. New draft: four-card tutorial deal, five-symbol replacement protocol and exact costs.

VARIABLE COSTS · PROPOSED RULESET

The team chooses its losses. The house keeps the names.

One cost buys progress

Success: earn the seal free. Failure: choose an offering, a curse, or a person. Then receive the seal/instruction and continue. One payment per challenge, no repeat-failure grind.

Opening tutorial: four shuffled cards, exactly one Veiled, randomly assign blindness immediately. This tutorial is separate from later failed-test payments. Captain has chosen random immediate blindness and the honest difficult card test; exact deck/round details are draft implementation.

Genuine puzzle outcomes, honestly shuffled ESP draws, compatible curse draws and group choices make different runs different. The honest five-symbol test is designed to fail almost always. Do not falsify a lucky success. The rule is five consecutive correct guesses in one five-draw attempt, not endless attempts until someone succeeds.

Blindness without plot armor

Veiled persists until that guest’s personal game ends. They can be sacrificed; if they remain, they finish blind. No immunity, fixed removal order or promised solo hero. Baseline healer can lift a curse; proposed limit excludes Veiled so a surviving blind guest still ends blind.

A temporary curse may not close every solution route. The operator offers only compatible cards; alternate sound, tactile, spoken or visual clues remain available. Mark the expiry on each card: one full subsequent challenge, except Veiled. Awarding the seal just paid for does not clear the new curse. The healer may cure it before that next attempt; otherwise it persists through that challenge’s resolution.

For a lone guest with no offering and no compatible ability curse, use The Named: surrender their name token to the house while retaining their abilities. This proposed identity curse makes the finale’s cost concrete without a deadlock.

Three things to keep separate

Spendable offerings: four shared supplied tokens. Lose one to avoid a curse or sacrifice. They are optional costs, not clues.

Required ritual inventory: four seals and four keepsakes. These always remain in the shared basket; departing guests leave all clues behind.

Name register: four guest-name tokens exist from arrival. A removed guest’s place is marked claimed; the final ritual claims every remaining place. Thus four lives are accounted for whether 1 or 4 players reach the chest.

Removal & exit

A person-payment is available only with at least two people in the game. Captain and Tamari enter together, escort the chosen guest through the side threshold, then return. The last guest cannot pay themself before the ending.

Removed guests receive a brief personal “you belong to the house” epilogue and a marked keepsake card, then return to the party. They do not wait to rejoin, operate machinery or perform a new role.

The Tory-spy Menace and Healer are both baseline. Additional Deafened, Silenced and No Hands curses remain in play. Proposed healer rule: one cure token removes one non-Veiled curse; it cannot bring a sacrificed player back. Preserving Veiled keeps the blind-ending requirement intact; the exact cure limit remains draft tuning. Child-antagonist play stays HELD.

Clear instructions: “Four seals. At a failed test, pay one cost and continue. Keep all clues in this basket.” Print the count, named zones and payment icons on the map. Explain sacrifices before play, but keep the mother’s ownership trap secret until the register reveal.
FEASIBILITY AT EVERY COUNT

One, two, three or four can finish.

4: everyone may remain by spending offerings or accepting curses. Share clue roles; the final claim turns all four names. Example: spend an offering on the ESP test, solve the real challenges.

2–3: one or two player-payments reduce available hands/eyes/ears. Remaining roles are transferable; operator alternates cover lost senses. The empty marked places make the missing guests part of the story.

1 sighted: all tasks have solo versions; no simultaneous multi-person locks. Ghost clues, taps and manual releases replace abilities temporarily lost to curses.

1 blind: raised grille edge + spoken letters; audible code; tactile star; raised-dot phrase tiles; voiced board letters and name-token reveal. Guided transitions, finale seated, no visual-only win condition.

HELD OPTION: removed guests return as the mother’s children to oppose survivors. Captain likes it but worries about novice complexity. Keep it out of the baseline; consider a single scripted knock/line only in a later test.

Pressure without invalidating success: helpful play can save people from early removal, while the last revelation still claims them. What changes is who experiences the climax and what they sacrificed on the way—not whether a genuine puzzle answer counts.

BASELINE ROLES & CURSES · CAPTAIN SELECTED

They have already dealt the mother’s hand.

The healer

A public overlay, distinct from the hidden spy. Can lift a curse. Draft tuning: one cure token; use it before a challenge to remove Deafened, Silenced or No Hands. Veiled persists to honor the blind-ending requirement. If the healer is sacrificed, the cure is lost unless used first; this is a real group tradeoff, not a plot requirement.

The Tory’s spy

The secret Menace serves the husband. A private instruction permits one false suggestion or a short deliberate delay, never hiding essential clues or obstructing a guest. They can be cursed or sacrificed too. The mother’s eventual trap includes the spy.

Curse compatibility: Deafened → others relay clues or use text/tactile versions. Silenced → gesture/tap. No Hands → direct another guest or use operator positioning. Veiled → spoken/tactile versions and guided movement. Curses are meaningful losses of participation, but not real confinement or a reason the show cannot finish.
