## Brainstorm
- Theme, loosely: **fortune tellers**.
- The captain and Tamari have talked out a rough story they intend to go with; there are also a few loose ideas for mechanisms.

### Format and constraints
- Bias toward **escape room** rather than pure haunted house.
- Everything **indoors** this year (2025 was partly outside: tent rigging, wind, garage lair).
- Fewer animatronics: the captain may lose about two weeks in October.
- So the experience must be **heavily narrative-driven and tight**.
- Still wants it **extravagant**, "really good" as always, but with limited build time: lean on **theater over robots**.

### Backstory (as told by the captain)
- Set in New England, Revolutionary era.
- A woman who was a revolutionary, with little formal power as a woman at the time.
- Her husband was a Tory (loyalist).
- She supported the revolution through subversive, covert means.
- She had four children.
- It went badly: all four of her children were murdered and her house was burned down.
- She does NOT become a fortune teller. She is **the ghost** - dead since the Revolution, about 250 years ago (fits the 2026 semiquincentennial).
- The game is played by **four players**, mirroring her four children.
- She gets the players to do things for her; her true aim is to **sacrifice the four players to resurrect her four children**.

### Where "fortune tellers" fits
- The fortune-teller theme is the **spiritualist/medium layer**, not a character: Ouija boards, seances, and similar themes.
- Arc of contact: early on, mediums help the players communicate with the ghost; later the players communicate with the ghost **more directly**.

## Earlier ideas (from before the storyline was chosen)
The captain flagged these as predating the chosen storyline; they may not fit it cleanly, but the storyline above is what they want to go with.

### Original concept: the lying ghost
- A ghost presents itself as a victim ("help me, I was murdered, help me find my killer"), but is actually malicious and trying to do something bad.

### Opening fortune-teller card reading
- A fortune teller at the very beginning does readings with fortune-telling cards that carry special game meanings.
- The four-person party must meet some minimum composition, set by the cards.
- **Curse cards:** if the party fails a challenge, they must use a curse card, which takes away one player's sense or ability:
  - blinded (safe version: mask/blindfold)
  - deafened (safe version: earphones/ear protection)
  - unable to speak
  - unable to use their hands
- **Menace card:** one team member is secretly a menace who tries to sabotage the group, or at least slow it down.
- **Explicit sacrifice:** at some point the team must deliberately sacrifice a member; the captain and Tamari come in and physically take that player out of the escape room.
- **Rigged challenges:** some challenges are deliberately impossible, forcing the team to spend a curse card.
- **"Treasure" cards (unresolved):** something the team must give up that doesn't directly handicap a player - an alternative cost to curse cards. The captain isn't sure where they landed on this.

### Challenges that complement the curses
Design principle: each challenge leans on one of the senses/abilities the curses remove (sight, hearing, speech, touch), so curses bite.
- **Hearing:** listen to a ghost whisper a clue.
- **Sight:** study horrific murder-scene photos (presumably the ghost's own murder scene) for clues.
- **Touch:** reach into something by feel - Orbeez were considered, but it needs more than one sensation: players get used to it, then later someone grabs their hand.
- **Example impossible challenge:** a Zener/ESP card test - someone holds up cards facing away and the player must guess them. Nobody can do it, so a curse card gets spent.
- **Hypnosis** as a possible element.
- **False choices everywhere:** players are made to feel they have choices, but none are real.
- **Ghost via fog machine** (e.g. an apparition in fog).
- **The promised prize:** everyone believes a great prize awaits at the end; the trick is the group ends up sacrificing everyone, and the "prize" is something terrible.
- **Treasures resolved (tentatively):** the "treasure" items were meant to be sacrifices/offerings players could bring with them, rather than rewards.
- **Saving people from a curse:** possibly a way to lift a curse off a player.
- **Healer role:** possibly one player is a healer (could pair with curse-lifting).

### Mechanism idea: the self-moving Ouija board
- The Ouija board spells out a clue.
- Players must keep their hands on the planchette; a camera running machine learning checks that hands are on it. If nobody is touching it, it won't move.
- The planchette is actually moved by a **magnet on a hidden Cartesian (XY) gantry** under the board.
- Possible extension (flagged by the captain as maybe too complicated): pictures of several ghosts; players place a ghost's picture or icon on the board to get that ghost's clue.

## Brainstorm: fitting the earlier mechanics into the colonial-mother story
Firstmate's suggestions, offered for discussion (not decided):
1. **Curses mirror how each child died.** Four children, four deaths - one blinded, one silenced, one deafened, one whose hands were bound or burned. Each curse card makes a player relive one child's death, which ties the sense-removal mechanic directly to the tragedy.
2. **Her spycraft becomes the puzzles.** Real Revolutionary tradecraft: invisible "sympathetic stain" ink (reveal with heat or UV), mask/grille letters (a cut-out overlay reveals the hidden message in an innocent letter - the British used these), number codebooks like the Culper Ring's, and signals hidden in household life (Anna Strong's laundry-line signals). Her subversion was hiding messages in a household her Tory husband controlled, so the house itself is the puzzle box.
3. **The menace is the Tory.** The menace card makes one player her husband's agent - the loyalist in the group - sabotaging from within, like he did to her.
4. **The treasures are the children's belongings.** A wooden toy, a sampler, a drum, a locket. Players think they are collecting clues or prizes; each item they pick up quietly binds them to one child - the offerings. The promised prize ("she'll lead you to the hidden Continental war chest") is the lure.
5. **The mediums are a later-era layer.** The house was rebuilt and a spiritualist medium now holds séances there - the opening card reading is the medium "assigning protections" (the curse cards), unknowingly doing the ghost's work. Ouija, séance, and hypnosis sit naturally in that spiritualist parlor.
6. **Period-true versions of the sense challenges.** Whisper from the cold hearth/chimney; instead of photos, an inquest broadside or charcoal sketches of the burning; touch challenge reaches into the ashes of the burned house (Orbeez as cold wet ash), then a small hand grabs yours.
7. **Impossible challenge swap:** Zener cards are 1930s - use the medium's tarot or playing cards held face-away for the same effect.
8. **The ending:** each sacrificed player is taken by the captain and Tamari (as the children, or as redcoats); the final reveal in the fog is the mother with her four children "restored" - the players.

## Brainstorm: séance, mesmerism, and puppetry effects (firstmate suggestions)
Period hook: mesmerism is exactly her era. Franz Mesmer was active in the 1770s-80s, and he used Benjamin Franklin's glass armonica to put patients into trance. Franklin later investigated Mesmer for the French king.
- **Mesmer's baquet:** a wooden tub with iron rods sticking out; patients hold the rods and each other's hands to form a "magnetic chain". As a mechanic: the effect (the ghost's voice, lights, the Ouija board waking up) only happens while all players hold the rods and hands - capacitive or low-voltage touch sensing. It forces the group together and pairs with curses (a player without hands breaks the chain).
- **Glass armonica tone:** the eerie singing-glass sound as the ghost's signature; a recording is fine, or real wine glasses.
- **Table rapping:** solenoid knockers hidden in the séance table and walls answer yes/no questions ("one knock yes, two no"), operated live by the captain or Tamari. Very theater, low build.
- **Table tipping:** a hidden linear actuator or an offstage lever tilts the séance table under their hands.
- **Spirit cabinet (Davenport brothers):** a curtained cabinet; bells and instruments play when the curtain closes. A person inside does it - pure theater.
- **Spirit hands in the dark:** a glove on a stick, or a real hand touching players during a dark séance - pairs with the touch challenge.
- **Slate writing:** a message "appears" on a sealed double school slate (pre-written, flap trick) - a clean clue delivery.
- **Ectoplasm and apparition:** cheesecloth "ectoplasm"; the ghost as a Pepper's-ghost reflection or a projection onto fog or scrim.
- **Mesmerism with a stooge:** the medium "mesmerizes" a volunteer. A plant (Tamari?), or a real player given a secret instruction card ("when you hear the bell, you are her child"), which doubles as the menace or false-choice device.
- Captain on the séance/mesmerism list: "Actually they're all pretty cool" - all candidates stay in; none dropped yet.
