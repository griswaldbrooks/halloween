What the drafts assumed

Filled in by me or the storyboard, never approved by you. Challenge any of it.

  • Nine scenes in a fixed order, about 28 minutes.
  • One puzzle per sense: grille letter (sight), hearth whisper (hearing), ash reach (touch), hypnosis phrase (speech).
  • Every failure is paid one of three ways: a curse, an offering token, or a person.
  • Four offering tokens, separate from the children's keepsakes.
  • The healer has one cure and cannot cure blindness.
  • A curse lasts through one more challenge.
  • A name register at the door sets up a personal reveal at the end.
  • The lure is an inheritance or hidden war chest.
  • The final "choice" is fake: both options complete the bargain.
  • Removed players go back to the party and take no further part.
  • The specific ways each child died.
  • Two performers run everything.

Finale research: five proposals

Full report: data/halloween-finale-research/report.md. Nothing here is decided.

What the keepsakes-and-graves idea was missing: the mother has nothing to do in it; the result is settled before the finale starts; the truth players uncovered is never needed; the Informer has no part; nothing happens after it.

  1. The roll call (recommended). Against the sand timer, the mother calls her children home by name, eldest first. For each, the group places that child's keepsake on the name plate and says aloud what really happened. The official story does nothing. Her version does nothing. Only the truth, naming her own part, puts the child to rest and frees the player tied to that child. When the sand runs out she takes whoever is left, by the child's name. Zero to four walk out.
  2. The four chairs. For players taken mid-game: they sit in the last room as her children, in small chairs, in plain sight. The survivors win each one back with the same rite, then call the friend by their real name. Pairs with the roll call.
  3. Buttons in the bag. Every curse puts one of your buttons in her sewing bag; giving up a keepsake puts in two. At the end she draws one button per child not at rest. Real chance, weighted by what you gave her.
  4. Closing the circle. Back at the séance table, each sitter returns a keepsake and tells the truth; candles go out one by one; ten seconds of full dark; the light returns on empty chairs. Strongest theater, weakest game.
  5. The Informer's last word. An accusation vote, then she offers the Informer the same deal the first informer took. Weakest for four novices.

Suggested extras: the medium states the rule in plain words just before the finale ("She does not want them found. She wants them replaced."); a slate by the door reading "Entered 4. Returned 2."; taken players wear the child's name for the rest of the party; she puts a coin in the Informer's hand as she leaves.

Tonight

Notes from this session land here as we talk.

  • Confirmed: format, the ghost and her story, four players for four children, mediums as the séance layer, curses take a sense.
  • Lore goal: a full-enough story of who she was, what she did, what happened to her, who she is as a ghost, and what she wants.
  • She was a Daughter of Liberty (the 1760s-70s group; boycotts, spinning bees, some spied). Her Tory husband hosts British officers; she listens at her own table and passes on what she hears. You: "exactly who I am picturing."
  • Name rule: first and last chosen together, and the last name must carry meaning. Not Torrey (sounds like Tory); Pratt is too neutral. Choose after the backstory.
  • Her spycraft (all with real precedents): listening at the table; invisible ink between the lines of ordinary letters; notes sewn into cloth-covered buttons on her children's coats, with the children as couriers. Other real references: clothesline signals, a number code book, memorizing and eating a dispatch.
  • Game hooks you spotted: a coat the players must search; an invisible-ink puzzle; the courier work gives a direct reason a child is killed.
  • Decided: each child had a job in her spy ring, one per sense, and each died through that same sense. Eldest carried the button notes (hands); one kept lookout (eyes); one listened at the officers' door (ears); the youngest memorized and recited messages (voice). Whether players are cursed one-for-one to match is still open.
  • Decided: she was betrayed by someone from her own spy ring. The menace card mirrors this: one player is the Informer. (This replaces the earlier idea that the menace is the Tory husband's agent.)
  • Decided, the night it ended: the husband is a true loyalist who believes in the Crown's justice. When the informer talks, he confirms it and hands over her letters, expecting an arrest and trial. The officers make an example instead: soldiers kill the children, each through the work they did, and burn the house. She is held outside and made to watch it burn. Then the British make him prove his loyalty by executing her himself, as justice for a spy.
  • Decided, the lie: as a ghost she says her husband murdered her children. In her mind that is true in effect. She hides that she made them couriers; players uncover it.
  • Liked, kept flexible: why now: 250 years since 1776, the one night the bargain can be made. The bargain: four lives were taken, so four lives willingly given bring her children back. She cannot simply take the players; they must agree step by step without knowing what they agree to, which is why she needs a lie and a lure.
  • Decided, why now: not the 250th year. She has been trapped, silent and in anguish because nobody could hear her. The house stands on the old burned foundation; something of hers turns up (for example a child's coat button with a note still sewn inside); a medium holds a séance here on Halloween, when the veil is thinnest; and exactly four guests sit at the table. First night she has a voice, a thin veil, and four people.
  • Decided, the mediums: sincere believers who genuinely want to contact the dead, not charlatans putting on a show. They do not know what she intends.
  • Going with, her ask: "I cannot find my children. Help me bring them back to me." Mediums and players hear a spirit wanting to be reunited with her children so all can rest. She means it literally: alive again, through the players. She never lies about what she wants, only about the cost and her own part. Each task is "find my eldest, find my lookout," and each ties a player to a child.
  • Decided, history in layers: (1) The official record the medium researched, written by the winners of that night: she was a spy, tried and convicted, and there was a terrible fire. (2) Her version: her husband murdered her children. (3) The truth players uncover piece by piece: the soldiers, the children killed through their work, the forced execution, the informer, and her sending them out as couriers. Each layer is more sinister than the last.
  • Children named: see the table above (draft, likely final).
  • The husband (draft): first name Constant (a real Puritan name; more likely to change than the children's). A magistrate, justice of the peace under the Crown: officers dine at his table, he believes in a fair trial, and he is forced to carry out her sentence himself. Magistrate confirmed.
  • Decided, his fate: he does not die. He lives out his life unmarried, having lost everything. She is furious that he lived.
  • Held: her name and the family surname (finalists Graves, Wake, Ashe). You expect to work the names into clues, so the choice waits for the game design.
  • Surnames: Graves, Wake and Ashe are not the only options. Stay open to others; pick whatever helps the game most.
  • Game, your direction: players go through together, not spread out. About eight or nine short games in pairs: you learn something in one, it is essential for the next, then the one after is independent. Short dependence chains only.
  • Pairs fit the lore: four children, two beats each (learn the job, then use it), plus a finale makes nine. Cautions agreed: about three minutes per game, so the learning beat is quick; and failure needs a way forward at a price.
  • Knowing they failed (proposed): a sand timer on the table that the medium turns over; sand runs out means failure. No electronics.
  • Curses and keepsakes (proposed, intriguing, not settled): each player gets a child's keepsake. Failure always brings a curse. The sincere medium warns "never give it to her"; the ghost offers to lift a curse in exchange for the keepsake. Your objection to the first version: players need a real reason to keep it.
  • Open, needs thought: the finale. A tally of who became a child is not a finale. It is a game, so winning and losing must be fun. Ideas so far: she calls the children home by name and takers are led off while keepers finish a last task short-handed; or players removed earlier reappear at the end as a surprise (you find that clunky).
  • Finale idea three (closer, still missing something): the children want to rest and she is holding them. Final game: put each keepsake with the right child's grave or name plate, using everything learned. All four right means the children rest and the group wins; each keepsake handed to her earlier is a child she keeps, and that player is taken.
