# Halloween Haunted-House / Escape-Game Design Handoff

**Working project:** Revolutionary-era haunted house / escape-room-style Halloween party experience  
**Designers:** Tamari and Griz  
**Assistant nickname during session:** Patience  
**Status:** Work in progress. This document captures the actual discussion, including ideas that were explored but **not** necessarily accepted.

---

## 1. What Tamari and Griz are trying to make

The experience is intended to sit somewhere between:

- an escape room,
- an immersive haunted house,
- a séance / Spiritualist encounter,
- and a narrative puzzle game.

A major design concern is that this must be a **game**, not merely a story that happens around the guests.

The designers specifically compared it to an escape room they recently played in which the objective was unambiguous: locate the enemy submarines and destroy them. There was no major branching choice required; the players either accomplished the mission or failed.

That is the standard they want here: a clear objective, concrete player actions, and an unmistakable success/failure condition, while still allowing the story to contain reveals and reversals.

The intended group discussed in detail is **four players**.

---

## 2. Historical / fictional premise

The story centers on an as-yet-unnamed colonial/Puritan woman around the Revolutionary era.

She is married to a **magistrate judge** who is a Tory/Loyalist.

Secretly, however, she supports the Revolution and operates as a **spy**.

Her four children participate in her spy network.

### The four children

The deliberately Puritan-style names are:

| Child | Spy function | Associated faculty/sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Deliverance** | 14-year-old courier carrying messages between locations | Hands |
| **Waitstill** | Lookout | Eyes / sight |
| **Mindwell** | Listens/eavesdrops for information | Ears / hearing |
| **Remember** | Memorizes and recites messages | Voice |

The hands / eyes / ears / voice structure is important because it offers both story symbolism and possible physical game mechanics.

---

## 3. The family tragedy

A neighbor betrays the mother and exposes her as a spy.

Because her husband is a magistrate and a Loyalist, he is placed in an impossible position. Authorities pressure him to confirm that his wife is a spy.

The leverage used against him is his children: he is led to believe that cooperating and condemning his wife will protect them.

He ultimately confirms/betrays her, effectively sentencing her to arrest and/or death.

However, because communication is slow, events have already overtaken his decision. Soldiers are already headed to the family home.

The house is burned, and through circumstances still to be finalized, **all four children die in or around the fire**.

The husband does **not** know that his attempt to save them has already failed.

He is then forced to participate in or carry out his wife's execution.

This creates an important tragic misunderstanding:

- The mother may die believing her husband simply betrayed her.
- The husband betrayed her specifically because he believed doing so would save their children.
- The children were already doomed/dead regardless.
- The mother may not even know, at the time of her death, what happened to the children.

The exact amount each character knows at death remains deliberately unresolved.

---

## 4. Present-day framing: the séance

In the present day, Spiritualists/mediums have heard a sanitized or incomplete version of the legend surrounding the house.

Tamari would likely serve as the live medium / Spiritualist host.

The initial séance is the players' entry into the story.

Possible introductory elements discussed:

- fortune-teller / divination cards,
- séance objects,
- relics or tokens,
- assigning players connections to individual children,
- curses that initially appear symbolic but become mechanically important later.

The medium does **not** know the complete historical truth.

That is intentional.

The players begin with the "PG" folklore version and uncover the much uglier reality through play.

---

## 5. A promising core dramatic structure

One of the strongest ideas developed during the conversation was that **the ghost of the mother is also learning the truth alongside the players**.

She is not an omniscient exposition device.

At the beginning, she may believe her children escaped, disappeared, or remain lost somewhere beyond her reach.

Therefore the apparent initial mission could be something like:

> Find my children. Discover what became of them.

Each major puzzle/chapter concerns one child and reveals both:

1. that child's role in the spy network, and
2. some portion of what ultimately happened to them.

As the players reconstruct the past, the mother reconstructs it too.

This allows her emotional state to evolve during the game instead of having her simply explain the plot.

---

## 6. The major tonal/gameplay turn

The designers liked the possibility that roughly the first three quarters of the experience are investigative.

Then the final discovery lands:

**All four children died.**

At that exact moment, the historical fire effectively begins happening again around the players.

This is intended to be a major sensory transformation of the space:

- fire/crackling audio,
- changing or flickering lighting,
- moving fire-like shadows,
- screaming in the sound design,
- fog/smoke-machine effects,
- escalating urgency.

The room is not literally burning; the haunting is causing the players to experience or reenact the catastrophe.

This moment should mark the transition from:

**"Discover what happened"**

to a new, urgent final objective.

Exactly what that final objective is remains the biggest unresolved design question.

---

## 7. The mother's transformation

Once the mother learns that all four children died, she may break down and become an **enraged ghost**.

Several possibilities were discussed but not finalized.

One darker concept from Tamari and Griz is that she might attempt to **resurrect her children**, possibly using the four living players as sacrifices or vessels.

That has a useful symmetry:

- four dead children,
- four living guests,
- four senses/faculties.

This could also explain why the séance or the mother marked/cursed the guests earlier.

However, the designers have **not committed** to this version.

Another possibility is that the mother's rage is based on an incomplete understanding: she learns that her husband betrayed her but does not yet know **why** he did it.

The final act could therefore involve uncovering the husband's true motive—that he condemned his wife because he believed doing so would save their children.

The key design problem is turning this revelation into **physical gameplay**, rather than merely reading a final letter or listening to exposition.

---

## 8. Curses / sensory impairments

The designers are interested in mechanically cursing the four players in ways corresponding to the children:

- sight → sleep mask / blindfold,
- hearing → earmuffs or other muffling,
- hands → bulky gloves or another dexterity limitation,
- voice → inability to speak.

An important design concern arose:

It would be awkward if, during the climax, the host suddenly had to stop everything and say out of character:

> "Okay Bob, put on these gloves. Alice, here's your blindfold..."

### Proposed solution

Make the curse apparatus part of the experience **from the beginning**.

At the séance, each player is assigned or receives an object associated with one child. The object initially seems ritualistic or symbolic.

The rules of the séance can establish something like:

> If the spirit calls you, obey it.

Later, when the haunting intensifies, the ghost can invoke each player/child and the players **activate their own curse**.

For example:

- an already-worn scarf becomes a blindfold,
- earmuffs/headphones are already part of a prop,
- gloves are already in someone's possession,
- a token/card instructs one person that they may no longer speak.

This preserves theatrical continuity: **the players curse themselves in response to the haunting** rather than the game masters pausing play to equip them.

### Safety note

If sight is actually blocked, that player should not be required to navigate an obstacle-filled or foggy room. The blindness mechanic should occur at a stationary puzzle/work area or otherwise be designed safely.

---

## 9. Relics / keepsakes

A recurring mechanical idea was that each child's section produces a **relic, keepsake, or token**.

These could be returned to:

- the séance table,
- a ritual board,
- four marked positions,
- or another central apparatus.

The fourth relic being placed could trigger the major transition:

1. the final truth becomes apparent,
2. the mother understands that her children died,
3. fire audio begins,
4. lights transform,
5. fog enters,
6. the curses activate,
7. the final phase begins.

This gives the designers a very concrete trigger for synchronized theatrical effects.

---

## 10. Finale mechanics explored during the conversation

Several finale ideas were proposed. **These are exploratory, not final.**

### Cross-sensory cooperation

The four curses can make the final puzzles impossible for any one player to solve alone.

For example:

- the person associated with sight cannot see,
- the person associated with hearing cannot hear,
- the hands player cannot perform fine manipulation,
- the voice player cannot speak.

Information can be distributed so players must compensate for one another's missing faculty.

This creates a thematic payoff: the four children operated as a network, and now the four guests must do the same.

### A timed fire sequence

The final phase could have a fixed soundtrack of perhaps ~10 minutes.

Rather than needing a visible countdown clock, the soundtrack itself can escalate toward catastrophe.

Puzzle successes could remove layers of the soundscape—for example, silencing an individual child's scream or changing some other audio element—giving immediate feedback.

### Concrete prop concepts that were floated

These examples were generated during brainstorming and should **not** be treated as locked designs:

**Deliverance / hands:**  
A courier satchel or locked container involving knots, cords, or tactile manipulation. The cursed Deliverance player may possess necessary interpretive information but lack dexterity because of gloves, requiring another player to operate the lock.

**Mindwell / hearing:**  
A locked box or device whose solution depends on whispered/spoken information embedded in the fire soundscape. Other players may need to hear and relay information to the hearing-impaired player.

**Waitstill / sight:**  
A lookout/orientation puzzle using directions, landmarks, patrol routes, windows, roads, church, woods, arrows, etc. The sight-impaired player possesses some critical knowledge while others must act as their eyes.

**Remember / voice:**  
A final spoken message, code phrase, testimony, or remembered statement that must ultimately be voiced, despite Remember's curse being silence.

The group specifically pushed back against vague puzzle descriptions. Future design work needs to specify **actual buildable props, exact clue content, exact solution logic, reset procedure, and what physically happens when solved.**

---

## 11. A funny but useful design warning: "314"

During an attempt to make a knot-counting puzzle concrete, Patience casually proposed a three-digit code of **3-1-4**.

Tamari and Griz immediately noticed that another AI they had previously discussed the project with had independently proposed the exact same arbitrary number.

Conclusion: do **not** use arbitrary AI-ish puzzle codes.

Every code/combination should arise organically from:

- the historical story,
- dates established in the fiction,
- names,
- spycraft,
- objects physically present,
- or information the players genuinely discover.

No unexplained 314s.

---

## 12. An ending concept that was explored but did NOT fully satisfy the designers

One proposal was:

- Players recover/rescue symbolic versions of all four children during the burning-house sequence.
- The children then speak to their mother via recorded voices.
- They reveal that their father did not simply abandon/betray them; he believed his confession would save them.
- The family is spiritually reconciled.
- The séance closes and everyone passes on.

This has emotional potential, but the designers correctly identified that it risks becoming **story after the gameplay has already ended**.

There was also momentary confusion over how many ghosts/actors this implied. The family consists of:

- mother,
- father,
- Deliverance,
- Waitstill,
- Mindwell,
- Remember,

for **six spirits total**.

The intent was *not* to require six live character actors; recorded voices, sound cues, or other theatrical devices could represent spirits. Tamari is currently envisioned as the principal live medium.

But the larger issue remains: the designers want the **last story revelation and the final player victory to be mechanically integrated**, rather than solving the last box and then watching an ending scene.

---

## 13. The most important unresolved question

The conversation ended on the recognition that the next design decision should probably come **before** building detailed finale puzzles:

### What does the mother actually try to do once she learns her children are dead?

And therefore:

### What happens if the players fail?

Those two answers determine the real win condition.

Possible directions currently on the table include:

**Resurrection / possession:**  
The mother intends to resurrect her four children using the four players as vessels or sacrifices. The players must prevent the ritual from completing.

**Rage / vengeance:**  
She concludes that her husband murdered/betrayed the family and attempts some destructive act. Players must uncover and prove the full truth before she completes it.

**Historical reenactment / trapped loop:**  
The haunting forces the house to relive the fire. Players must alter, complete, or symbolically resolve something that was left unfinished before the loop reaches its fatal conclusion.

**Rescue:**  
Players somehow "get the children out" of the supernatural recreation of the burning house. This is mechanically clear, but needs stronger logic about what rescue means when the children are already historically dead.

These could potentially be combined, but the finale should ultimately have **one sentence that tells players what they must accomplish**.

---

## 14. Desired design principles going forward

Future work should preserve these principles that emerged from the discussion:

1. **Clear objective.** Players should understand what they are trying to accomplish, particularly during the finale.

2. **Success and failure should be legible.** It should be possible to say plainly, "You succeeded because you did X before Y happened."

3. **Story revelations should change gameplay.** Avoid long stretches where players merely listen to lore after puzzles are complete.

4. **The mother's ignorance is valuable.** Let her discover the tragedy alongside the players.

5. **The fire is the climax transition.** The revelation of the children's deaths and the sensory transformation of the room should coincide.

6. **Use the four senses mechanically.** Hands, eyes, ears, and voice should matter beyond symbolism.

7. **Curses should feel diegetic.** Ideally players activate/accept their own impairments because of rules established earlier, avoiding an out-of-character equipment break.

8. **Four-player cooperation should be essential.** The final phase should make the players depend on one another.

9. **Props need to be genuinely buildable.** Future proposals should specify exactly what the players see, touch, hear, manipulate, enter, unlock, carry, or say.

10. **Puzzle answers should come from the fiction.** No arbitrary codes.

11. **The ending must still be a game.** Emotional resolution is welcome, but it cannot substitute for a final playable objective.

# Condensed Story Timeline

For handing this to another designer/AI quickly:

**Past:**  
A Puritan/colonial woman married to a Tory magistrate secretly spies for the Revolution. Her four children—Deliverance, Waitstill, Mindwell, and Remember—form part of her spy network as courier/hands, lookout/eyes, listener/ears, and memorizer/voice. A neighbor betrays her. Authorities force her husband to confirm her treason by threatening the children. He condemns her because he believes this will save them. Unknown to him, soldiers are already attacking the home. The children die in the fire. The mother is executed, possibly never knowing what became of them or why her husband betrayed her.

**Present:**  
Tamari, acting as a Spiritualist/medium, conducts a séance based on an incomplete popular version of the legend. Four guests are connected to the four children and receive ritual objects/tokens/curses. The mother asks them to discover what became of her children.

**Investigation:**  
Four child-centered puzzle sequences gradually reconstruct the spy ring and each child's fate. Relics/keepsakes are recovered and brought to a central ritual location.

**Turn:**  
The fourth discovery proves that all four children died. The mother learns this simultaneously with the guests. She breaks down. The historical fire manifests around them through sound, lighting, shadows, screaming, and fog. The four sensory curses activate.

**Finale:**  
Still under design. It should be an urgent, highly cooperative physical sequence involving the four impaired faculties and the truth about the husband. The mother's post-revelation intention must be defined first, because that determines the players' final objective and failure condition.

# Best next design session

Do **not** start by inventing four more puzzles.

First answer these three questions:

### 1. What does the mother do when she learns the children are dead?

Write this as an active sentence:

> "She now intends to ______."

### 2. What will happen in ten minutes if nobody stops her?

Write the failure state physically and visibly:

> "If the players fail, ______."

### 3. What physical action constitutes victory?

Not "learn the truth" or "bring peace." Something performable:

> "The players win when they ______."

Once those three sentences are good, build the final puzzle chain backward from the winning action.

That is where the October 7 design session left off.
